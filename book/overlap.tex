\chapter{Failure of the cavity method}
\label{ch:overlap}

Every calculation in Parts~II and~III rests on one assumption, stated in
Sec.~\ref{sec:treelike} and repeated whenever it mattered: complexes meet in at
most one node. This part is about what to do when they do not.

Two complexes sharing a single node satisfy the assumption: the incidence
structure stays a tree and the recursions handle it. The case at issue is the
pair sharing \emph{two or more} nodes, which is what \emph{non-trivial overlap}
means here and in the two chapters that follow. Section~\ref{sec:price} draws
it.

The part runs one model, the Ising model, on three classes of network, and three
calculations on each. This chapter runs the cavity recursion on the chygraph,
$\mathrm{BP}_{\chi}$, and measures how wrong it is.
Chapter~\ref{ch:gbp} runs generalised belief propagation, the repair the
literature already has, and finds it does not recover the failure.
Chapter~\ref{ch:metacomplex} runs the cavity recursion on the merged chygraph,
$\mathrm{BP}_{\chi_{m}}$, which is exact on every real network and every
hyperbolic instance measured here, and which has a threshold beyond which it is
not.

The assumption buys the whole apparatus, and on the networks
Chapter~\ref{ch:data} measured it is false.
Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech} located where it enters, in the step where a node adds
what its complexes have told it as though they were independent.

\section{Non-trivial overlap}
\label{sec:price}
\index{treelike}
\index{overlap!between complexes}

Chapter~\ref{ch:chygraphs} states the central move: a triangle is a loop, a
tree-based calculation cannot carry one, so promote the triangle to a complex
and sum over its interior exactly, and the loop leaves the outside view.

Chapter~\ref{ch:ising} measured what that buys. It does not remove loops; it
moves them up one level. Two triangles sharing an
\emph{edge} are a loop in the incidence structure --- a cycle through complex,
node, complex, node --- exactly as a triangle is a loop in a graph. The chygraph
cannot see it, for the same reason and by the same argument
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sharededge}).

Chygraphs therefore move the boundary of what counts as local, from ``no
loops'' to ``no loops \emph{between} complexes''. Chapter~\ref{ch:cover}
measured that gain on hyperbolic random graphs: the degree-matched control has
no core at all, and the chygraph has very nearly the right one.


\section{Three classes of network}
\label{sec:threeclasses}
\index{hyperbolic random graph}
\index{Karrer--Newman model}
\index{Netzschleuder}

The rest of this part measures one model --- the Ising model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ising-intro}, a coupling $\beta J$ on every edge and no field ---
on three classes of network. They are chosen so that the clustering coefficient
does not separate them and the \emph{origin} of the triangles does.

\paragraph{Hyperbolic random graphs, where the triangles are grown.} Nodes are
placed in a disc of hyperbolic geometry and joined when they are close
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hrg-data}). The ensemble carries a power-law degree distribution
with a tunable exponent, strong clustering that follows from the geometry rather
than being inserted by hand, and a degree-matched configuration model available
as a control. Nothing chooses the triangles; the curvature produces them.

\paragraph{The Karrer--Newman ensemble, where the triangles are placed.} One
specifies, per vertex, how many single edges it carries and how many copies of
each chosen subgraph; the edges are matched in pairs and each subgraph's corners
in tuples, exactly as a configuration model matches stubs
\citep{karrer2010}. Clustering is whatever the motif density says it is, and can
be set as high as one likes.

Two placed motifs may share a vertex freely. They share an \emph{edge} only when
their remaining corners coincide, which for a random matching has probability
$O(1/n)$. No two complexes therefore meet in more than one atom, up to
coincidences whose number does not grow with the network. That is
Sec.~\ref{sec:treelike}'s pairwise condition, satisfied by construction, and it
is what leaves the merge closure of Sec.~\ref{sec:merging} with nothing to
merge.

It is not the absence of loops. The motifs are wired together by a
configuration model, and the incidence structure of that wiring carries a
$2$-core above an incidence branching of one, exactly as an Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi
graph carries cycles above mean degree one. That threshold is classical --- the
$k$-core transition of \citet{pittel1996} at $k=2$, and the point at which
\citeauthor{bauer2001}'s standard leaf removal stops consuming the graph
\citep{bauer2001} --- and it is where belief propagation stops being exact
\citep{mezard2009}. What Sec.~\ref{sec:coretransition} adds is where it sits in
this ensemble's own parameters; the instances used here are drawn well above it.

The two thresholds this book quotes are the two removal rules of that one paper.
Deleting a leaf and its edge leaves nothing above mean degree one; deleting a
leaf \emph{and its neighbour} leaves nothing until $\ave{k}=e$. Part~IV runs
the first, because a coupling determines an atom and does not eliminate its
complex, and Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} runs the second, because a covering
constraint does. That is why $P_{C}(\mathrm{Ising},\mathrm{BP}_{\chi})$ and
$P_{C}(\mathrm{VC},\mathrm{BP}_{\chi})$ are different numbers on the same
object.

\paragraph{Real networks, where the triangles are whatever was recorded.} Six
networks from Netzschleuder, in two families divided by how an edge came to be
recorded. In the first the data records \emph{groups} --- an author list, a
scene, a band --- and every group is a clique of its members by construction. In
the second, edges were recorded one pair at a time --- a friendship, a contact,
a fixture --- and the triangles are whatever homophily or proximity produced.
Call the two \emph{group-generated} and \emph{dyadic}. Their clustering
coefficients overlap, $0.57$ to $0.74$ against $0.40$ to $0.64$, which is what
makes the comparison worth making.

Because $\ln Z$ is evaluated by enumeration, instances are capped at twenty
vertices throughout. For the first two ensembles that is a size; for the real
networks it means neighbourhoods rather than whole networks, a vertex with its
neighbours induced. Table~\ref{tab:knfinite} shows the cap is not harmless on
the placed ensemble.

\section{The cavity calculation}
\label{sec:cavityfail}

Start with the smallest instance that has the defect at all. Couple every pair
inside each triangle of Fig.~\ref{fig:sharededge} --- the shared bond carrying
one coupling and not one per triangle, so that the object is the five-edge graph
the two triangles make --- and evaluate $\ln Z$ exactly by summing over all
sixteen configurations, and compare it with the counting the whole of Parts~II
and~III uses, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} (Table~\ref{tab:cavitytwo}).

The second column is the cavity method itself, run to a fixed point on the
clique chygraph: messages between atoms and complexes, each complex summing over
its own interior exactly, and $\ln Z$ read off the Bethe free energy of the
incidence structure. It is what Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech} prescribes, applied to
an object that violates its assumption. On a chygraph whose complexes meet in at
most one node the same recursion returns the exact answer to machine precision,
which is checked on chains, stars and trees of triangles before any of the
numbers below are quoted.

The error is one-signed, and the reason is the one Fig.~\ref{fig:sharededge}
draws. Equation~\eqref{eq:bethe} assigns $-1$ to node $1$ and $-1$ to node $2$
separately. Summing the counting numbers of every region that contains the pair
$\{1,2\}$ gives $2$, not $1$: the bond between the two shared nodes is counted
twice, once inside each triangle, and nothing subtracts it back out. The
calculation treats the two complexes as independent given their shared nodes,
and they are not, because the shared nodes are joined to each other.
Overestimating the number of available configurations overestimates $\ln Z$,
which is why every entry is positive.

All $240$ runs reach a fixed point. On hyperbolic random graphs the
error runs from $3.8\times10^{-2}$ to $61.2$ in $\ln Z$, with a median of
$2.72$; on the placed ensemble from $1.2\times10^{-2}$ to $24.7$, median $0.29$;
and on the real neighbourhoods from $8\times10^{-3}$ to $447$, median $5.06$. A
median of five in $\ln Z$ is a factor of $150$ in $Z$.

Reaching a fixed point is not the same as reaching \emph{the} fixed point, and
on two of the $120$ real neighbourhoods it is not: regenerating the measurement
lands them on a different one, at $58.1$ and $101.8$ in $\ln Z$ rather than
$116.6$ and $167.0$, with nothing in either iteration to say which was found.
Both are neighbourhoods of hospital contact --- a dyadic network
(Sec.~\ref{sec:threeclasses}), and one of the two least chordal in the set. It
changes no range, median or conclusion here --- the errors stay large and stay
one-signed --- and Sec.~\ref{sec:cavity-checks} gives the detail, but it is
worth saying at the point the numbers are quoted rather than only in the checks:
on real neighbourhoods a small residual does not by itself certify an answer,
which is the same thing Sec.~\ref{sec:realoverlap} will find one method later.

These are not instances on which propagation had an easy question to answer.
Figure~\ref{fig:corebonds} plots
$P_{C}(\mathrm{Ising},\mathrm{BP}_{\chi})$, the core fraction of the clique
chygraph under the removal this model licenses. An atom lying in one complex
leaves and a complex left holding one atom leaves with it, and what survives is
what the messages must resolve for themselves. Both arguments earn their place:
Figure~\ref{fig:core} plots $P_{C}(\mathrm{VC},\mathrm{BP}_{\chi})$ on the same
kind of object, and it is a different number, because a covering constraint lets
leaf removal take the complex around a forced atom and a coupling does not. Not one of the $120$ instances is
core-free, and
the medians are $0.59$ on the hyperbolic ensemble, $0.75$ on the placed one and
$0.67$ on the real neighbourhoods. On every instance the recursion is left with
a core, which is why it errs on every instance.

The error tracks the quantity Fig.~\ref{fig:sharededge} identifies, and tracks
it better than any coarser measure of overlap. Its logarithm correlates with the
fraction of bonds lying inside two or more complexes at $+0.65$, $+0.63$ and
$+0.83$ on the three classes. That fraction is the direct count of the defect:
a bond inside two complexes is summed by both, and its interaction enters the
free energy twice. Counting complex \emph{pairs} instead --- the
$\mathrm{shared}_{2+}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:data}, the fraction of intersecting
pairs that meet in two or more atoms --- also correlates, and less well on every
one of the three: $+0.58$, $+0.52$ and $+0.68$. What predicts the damage is how
much of the graph is double-counted, not how many complexes happen to intersect.

\section{The cause is the non-trivial overlap}
\label{sec:deficit}

The error above is attributed to overlap. That attribution is a measurement, not
an inference, and it is the \emph{rewired control} of Sec.~\ref{sec:hrg}.

Take the measured clique ensemble and rearrange it so that complexes meet in at
most one node, holding the ensemble fixed and changing only the arrangement. The
prediction agrees with that control to $10^{-3}$. The map is therefore right on
its own terms, and the whole remaining discrepancy against the real graph is
overlap. Without the control a wrong recursion and a violated assumption give
the same signature.

The same deficit shows outside the Ising model. On hyperbolic random graphs with
maximal cliques as complexes, Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} finds the chygraph
accounting for $77$ to $100$ per cent of the leaf-removal core, the shortfall
tracking the clique overlap: negligible in a sparse graph where cliques rarely
share edges, irrelevant once almost everything is core, largest in between.

\section{Checks}
\label{sec:cavity-checks}

\checkhead{Known results}
The cavity recursion and the Bethe free energy it stationarises are standard, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech} derives them. The implementation used here is checked
where the answer is known independently: on chygraphs whose complexes meet in at
most one node --- chains, stars, two triangles glued at a vertex, trees of
triangles --- it returns the exact $\ln Z$ to $2\times10^{-15}$ at every
coupling tried. That is the check that what the chapter then reports is the
overlap and not the solver. The free energy is evaluated in its explicit form,
with the factor--complex edge terms kept; folding them into a $1-k_{v}$
coefficient on the node terms is valid only when the messages are unnormalised,
and doing it wrongly costs whole multiples of $\ln 2$.

\checkhead{New results}
The measurement of what the violation costs is new. The two-triangle table
compares the cavity fixed point with an exact sum over sixteen configurations,
and the counting numbers are checked for what they are supposed to guarantee:
every node covered once, and the shared \emph{bond} covered twice, which is the
defect the table then prices. The three-ensemble figure repeats that comparison
on $240$ instances, and its exact column is enumeration throughout. All $240$
reach a fixed point, so no number here rests on an iteration that failed to
settle. The correlation is reported against the fraction of \emph{bonds} lying
in two or more complexes rather than the fraction of intersecting complex pairs,
because the first is the defect and the second only a proxy for it; the two give
$+0.65$, $+0.63$, $+0.83$ against $+0.58$, $+0.52$, $+0.68$, the bond measure
winning on every class. Both are printed by \code{figs/overlap.py} from the one
file, so the comparison cannot drift apart.

\checkhead{Partial results}
Taking maximal cliques as complexes presumes the clique-size distribution has a
limit, that is, that the excess cardinality $\ave{\sbar}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sbaru} converges. Against a degree-matched control at identical
degree sequence and seed, the paired ratio
$\ave{\sbar}_{\mathrm{HRG}}/\ave{\sbar}_{\mathrm{control}}$ is a converged
constant between $1.8$ and $4.8$ for $\tau\ge2.5$, flat over $n=10^{3}$ to
$3\times10^{5}$. At $\tau=2.1$ both diverge and the control diverges faster, so
below $\tau=2.5$ the measured ensemble is not converged and neither is anything
built on it. The instances here are drawn at $\tau=2.5$.

The two non-unique fixed points of Sec.~\ref{sec:cavityfail}, in detail.
Regenerating \code{probe/results/cavity\_clique.json} reproduces $238$ of the
$240$ runs to machine precision and lands the other two, both hospital contact
at $\beta J=0.3$, on a different fixed point. Both states pass the residual
test at $10^{-13}$, and a second regeneration reproduces the first exactly, so
the ambiguity is in the problem and not in the arithmetic of a given run.
Neither instance is the largest or the median error of its class, so the only
quoted number that moves is the third correlation: $+0.83$ on the regenerated
file against $+0.84$ on the one it replaced, which is why it is quoted to two
digits and not three. Nothing else in the file changes.

This part works with the Ising model throughout, and does so by choice: one
model across three ensembles isolates the effect of where the triangles come
from, which three models on one ensemble would not. The \emph{criterion} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:gbp} --- exact when the region family is a junction tree --- is
a statement about factor coverage and holds whatever lives on the edges. Every
\emph{magnitude} quoted in Part~IV is a magnitude for a pairwise model at two
couplings, and nothing measured here licenses carrying those numbers over to a
model with hard constraints. The leaf-removal deficit of
Sec.~\ref{sec:deficit} is the one non-Ising quantity in the part, and it is
inherited from Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} rather than recomputed.
