\title{Statistical mechanics on chygraphs}

\author{Alexei V\'azquez}
\affiliation{}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The Bethe--Peierls treatment of statistical-mechanics models on random graphs
with arbitrary degree correlations [V\'azquez and Weigt, Phys.\ Rev.\ E
\textbf{67}, 027101 (2003)] assumes the graph is locally treelike, and so cannot
see clustering. Chygraphs remove that assumption by promoting dense motifs to
complexes. I show that the chygraph percolation map is already a Bethe--Peierls
cavity recursion specialised to a scalar message, and that the extension to
general Hamiltonians is a single substitution: the argument of a generating
function becomes a measure and the product becomes a convolution. Three
consequences follow in closed form. The threshold tensor of the percolation
calculation becomes a stability tensor once its first moments are reweighted by
the mean derivative of the field update, giving the replica-symmetric and de
Almeida--Thouless lines for higher-order structures. A triangle solved exactly
inside itself transmits more per neighbour than an independent edge, so at
matched mean degree clustering raises the Ising critical temperature by
$14.5\%$ --- an effect invisible to the degree-and-correlation ensemble.
Minimum hitting set on a hypergraph has a replica-symmetry-breaking point at
$\ave{k}(c-1)=e$ for every fixed cardinality $c$, and cardinality heterogeneity
postpones it while correlation across cardinalities brings it forward. Finally,
leaf-removal core percolation is a chygraph fixed point with a three-state
message, which quantifies the clustering that a degree-matched configuration
model cannot reproduce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A statistical-mechanics model on a random graph can be solved by the
Bethe--Peierls scheme whenever the graph is locally treelike. Reference
\cite{VW03} carried that programme to graphs with an arbitrary degree
distribution \emph{and} arbitrary correlations between the degrees of adjacent
vertices, and used it to show that such correlations change the solution
structure of a hard optimisation problem qualitatively: minimal vertex covers
on strongly assortative scale-free graphs sit behind a replica-symmetry-breaking
transition, and simple heuristics stop finding them. The ensemble there is
specified by two objects, the degree distribution $p_d$ and the joint
edge-degree distribution $e_{dd'}$.

That pair does not encode clustering, and the derivation assumes it away. The
assumption is not innocuous. Hyperbolic random graphs, run through the same
leaf-removal heuristic, retain an extensive core at \emph{every} mean degree,
with no transition at all; their degree-matched configuration model --- the same
$p_d$, the same assortativity to within a few percent --- is core-free until the
mean degree reaches $4.5$--$10$, depending on the tail exponent. The feature
that separates them is the clustering that hyperbolic geometry induces, and it
lies outside $\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$ by construction.

Chygraphs \cite{vazquez2023pre,vazquez2024comnet} are built to remove exactly
that assumption. A chygraph is a set of complexes whose vertices are themselves
complexes; a dense motif --- a triangle, a clique, a hyperedge --- is promoted to
a complex, its interior is solved exactly, and the chygraph \emph{over}
complexes is treelike even when the underlying graph is not. Graphs,
hypergraphs, multiplex hypergraphs and interacting hypergraphs all map onto the
construction, and percolation on all of them reduces to one self-consistency
map whose linearisation is a single tensor.

This paper makes the connection precise and then uses it. Section
\ref{sec:correspondence} shows that the chygraph percolation map \emph{is} a
Bethe--Peierls cavity recursion, specialised to the one message type percolation
needs, and that the extension to a general Hamiltonian is a single substitution:
the argument of a generating function becomes a measure and the product becomes
a convolution. The sections that follow are what that buys.

The threshold tensor of the percolation calculation carries first moments
because in percolation the derivative of the field update is a constant.
Reweighting those moments by $\ave{u'}$ or $\ave{u'^2}$ turns it into the
replica-symmetric or de Almeida--Thouless stability tensor of an arbitrary model
on an arbitrary chygraph, with the index structure, the determinant and the
Perron root unchanged (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}). Solving a triangle exactly
inside itself then gives a transmission $t/(1-t+t^2)$ per neighbour rather than
$t$, so at matched mean degree clustering raises the Ising critical temperature
by $14.5\%$ --- between two ensembles that are identical in $\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$.

Section \ref{sec:fixedpoint} moves the same Jacobian off the trivial fixed point,
which separates finding a solution from diagnosing it, and recovers the
replica-symmetry-breaking line of Ref.~\cite{VW03} as a computed quantity rather
than as a failure to converge. Section \ref{sec:hittingset} lifts the vertex
cover problem itself to hypergraphs. Section \ref{sec:core} writes leaf-removal
core percolation --- the order parameter the hyperbolic measurement is about ---
as a chygraph fixed point, which requires a three-state message rather than a
scalar. Sections \ref{sec:population} and \ref{sec:freeenergy} carry the field
distributions and the free energy, and Sec.~\ref{sec:regions} states the price:
a chygraph is treelike only when its complexes meet in at most one node, and on
a hyperbolic random graph the cliques do not. Measured against a control that
holds the clique ensemble fixed, the chygraph accounts for $65$--$83\%$ of the
core. Chygraphs move the loop problem up one level; they do not remove it.

\section{The correspondence}
\label{sec:correspondence}

The cavity recursion of Ref.~\cite{VW03}, Eq.~(4),
\begin{equation}
  h^{(i|j)} = \mu + \sum_{k\neq j} u\!\left(h^{(k|i)}\right),
  \label{eq:vw}
\end{equation}
and the chygraph self-consistency conditions of Ref.~\cite{vazquez2023pre},
\begin{align}
  Q^{ml}_- &= \prod_k \Phi^l_k\!\left(Q^{lk}_-\right)
              \prod_k \Big[\bar G^l_k\Big]_{k=m}\!\!\left(Q^{lk}_+\right),
  \label{eq:Qm}\\
  Q^{ml}_+ &= \prod_k \Big[\bar\Phi^l_k\Big]_{k=m}\!\!\left(Q^{lk}_-\right)
              \prod_k G^l_k\!\left(Q^{lk}_+\right),
  \label{eq:Qp}
\end{align}
are the same object. Both carry a message on a directed link --- an edge in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vw}, an inclusion in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Qm}--\eqref{eq:Qp} --- updated
from every incoming message except the one it came from, the exclusion written
as $k\neq j$ in one case and $[\bar F]_{k=m}$ in the other. Both are exact on a
locally treelike structure and both assume replica symmetry.

Table~\ref{tab:dictionary} gives the correspondence. The one substantive
difference is the message itself: a probability generating function
$\Phi(x)=\sum_n p_n x^n$ evaluated at a scalar becomes, when the argument is a
measure on fields, $\sum_n p_n P^{*n}$, the $n$-fold convolution. That is
Eq.~(10) of Ref.~\cite{VW03} written as a generating function, and it is the
whole content of the extension.

\section{The stability tensor}
\label{sec:stability}

The tensor $A$ of Ref.~\cite{vazquez2023pre} has entries
$\ave{\kappa}_{lk},\ave{\kbar}_{lk},\ave{s}_{lk},\ave{\sbar}_{lk}$: first
moments, and nothing else. They are first moments because in percolation the
derivative of the field update is a constant, the occupation probability, and
thinning has already folded it into $\ave{\kappa}_{0l}=p\ave{k}$. Nothing else
multiplies a branch.

For a general Hamiltonian the linearised recursion is
\begin{equation}
  \delta h^{(i|j)} = \sum_{k\neq j} u'\!\left(h^{(k|i)}\right)\,\delta h^{(k|i)},
  \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
so every branch carries a factor $u'$. At the trivial fixed point that factor
is one number per traversal type, and the reweighting is therefore a pure
elementwise multiplication of the four moment tables. The $2L^2$ index
structure, the block layout, $\theta=-\det A$ and $\Lambda=\max\mathrm{eig}(-A)$
are unchanged. Two instabilities share the tensor and differ only in the weight,
\begin{equation}
  \ave{\kappa}\to\ave{\kappa}\ave{u'}, \qquad
  \ave{\kappa}\to\ave{\kappa}\ave{u'^2},
  \label{eq:reweight}
\end{equation}
the first giving the ferromagnetic or replica-symmetric instability and the
second the de Almeida--Thouless line.

Where the weights go is fixed by the two kinds of step a chygraph has. Moving
\emph{up} a chy-degree, a complex reports its own cavity field to a complex
that includes it: a pure relay, weight $1$. Moving \emph{down} into a complex,
the interaction transforms the field, and the weight is $u'$. Percolation is
the case $u'=p$ on both, and the construction returns $A$ entry for entry.

The recovered cases are the ones that should be recovered. A configuration-model
graph gives $\ave{\kbar}p=1$, the Molloy--Reed threshold; the ferromagnetic
Ising model on the same graph gives $\ave{\kbar}\tanh\beta J=1$; and squaring
the weight gives $\ave{\kbar}\tanh^2\beta J=1$, the spin-glass line.

One caveat on the factorisation. The correct entry is $\ave{\sbar\,u'}$, a joint
average over the internal structure of the complex; writing it as
$\ave{\sbar}\ave{u'}$ assumes $u'$ is the same for every complex in the layer.
That is less a restriction than a definition of what a layer is --- a class of
complexes with the same statistical properties --- and when $u'$ depends on
cardinality the remedy is to split the layer by cardinality, the same device
used for hyperdegree--cardinality correlation in Ref.~\cite{vazquez2024comnet}.

\subsection{A complex solved exactly inside itself}
\label{sec:triangle}

The field a complex emits is not a modelling choice. For a complex holding $c$
members with internal energy $-\beta H(\sigma)$, the field on member $0$ given
cavity fields $h_1,\dots,h_{c-1}$ on the others is
\begin{equation}
  u=\tfrac12\ln\frac{Z(\sigma_0=+1)}{Z(\sigma_0=-1)},\quad
  Z(\sigma_0)=\!\!\sum_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_{c-1}}\!\!
  e^{-\beta H(\sigma)+\sum_j h_j\sigma_j},
  \label{eq:emitted}
\end{equation}
summed exactly. This is the step percolation never has to take: reachability
factorises, so a complex can be compressed into a single component-size
generating function, and a general interaction cannot.

For a $c$-clique with coupling $J$, writing $t=\tanh\beta J$,
\begin{align}
  c=2:&\quad u'=t, \nonumber\\
  c=3:&\quad u'=\frac{t}{1-t+t^{2}}, \nonumber\\
  c=4:&\quad u'=\frac{t+t^{3}}{1-2t+3t^{2}},
  \label{eq:uprime}
\end{align}
with no useful closed form beyond that, but exact by enumeration for any
cardinality that can be enumerated. The triangle value exceeds $t$ on
$0<t<1$ and reduces to $t$ at leading order: a triangle is two independent
edges at high temperature and strictly better than two independent edges below
it. Clustering helps ferromagnetic order, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime} is how much.

The critical temperature follows for an arbitrary chygraph. Arriving at a node
through a layer-$l$ complex, it belongs to $\ave{\kbar}_m$ further layer-$m$
complexes when $m=l$ and $\ave{\kappa}_m$ otherwise, each offering $c_m-1$ other
members, so the branching matrix on the $L$ complex layers is
\begin{equation}
  B_{lm}=\Big[\ave{\kbar}_m\delta_{lm}+\ave{\kappa}_m(1-\delta_{lm})\Big]
         (c_m-1)\,u'_m,
  \label{eq:branch}
\end{equation}
and the transition is $\det(I-B)=0$. The linear algebra is $L\times L$ however
large the complexes are. For links and triangles this is
\begin{equation}
  \ave{\kbar}_L t+2\ave{\kbar}_T u'_T
  +2t\,u'_T\!\left(\ave{\kappa}_L\ave{\kappa}_T-\ave{\kbar}_L\ave{\kbar}_T\right)=1,
  \label{eq:LT}
\end{equation}
the last term vanishing for Poisson layers.

Table~\ref{tab:tc} is the consequence. Hold the mean number of neighbours fixed
and move them from links into triangles: $T_c$ rises, by $20.6\%$ at four
neighbours and $4.8\%$ at twenty, the effect being $O(t^2)$ and so decaying as
$1/\ave{k}$. The two ensembles compared in each row have the same degree
distribution and the same assortativity. They are indistinguishable in
$\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$.

\section{Stability of the non-trivial fixed point}
\label{sec:fixedpoint}

The published tensor is the Jacobian at $Q=1$, the trivial fixed point, whose
Perron root is $1+\Lambda$. Evaluated instead at the fixed point the map
actually reaches, the same Jacobian answers a different question --- is the
solution stable? --- and for percolation the answer is
\begin{equation}
  \rho\big(J(Q^\ast)\big)=1-\Lambda+O(\Lambda^2),
  \label{eq:exchange}
\end{equation}
verified to $0.15\%$ at $\Lambda=1.1\times10^{-3}$. The two roots move away from
$1$ at the same rate in opposite directions: an exchange of stability, made
quantitative.

One diagnostic does not survive the move and is worth naming, because it is
tempting. The sign of the leading eigenvalue does \emph{not} distinguish a
monotone map from an anti-monotone one. The coupled core of a chygraph
alternates between up and down message roles, so $J$ is a non-negative matrix of
period two and Perron--Frobenius places eigenvalues at both $+\rho$ and $-\rho$.
The signs of the \emph{entries} are the unambiguous test: non-negative for
generating functions, non-positive for the hard-core maps below.

That distinction is the whole difficulty of the vertex-cover problem of
Ref.~\cite{VW03}. In the $\mu\to\infty$ limit its Eq.~(16) reads
$\pi_d=\sum_{d'}p(d'|d)(1-\pi_{d'})^{d'}$, which is order-\emph{reversing}:
raising any message lowers every message. Plain iteration oscillates, and past
the replica-symmetry-breaking point it never settles --- which is precisely how
Ref.~\cite{VW03} detects the transition. Under its correlation model
$e_{dd'}=q_d[r\delta_{dd'}+(1-r)q_{d'}]$ the map closes on a single scalar, so
bisecting on that scalar reaches the fixed point whether or not it is stable,
and the Jacobian is then asked about it separately. Two instruments in place of
one: the solution is found, and the diagnosis is computed.

Doing so reproduces Fig.~1 of Ref.~\cite{VW03} --- $x_c$ rising with $r$, the
$\gamma=2.5$ curve below the $\gamma=3.0$ one, uncorrelated scale-free graphs
replica-symmetric --- and locates the breaking at $r=0.70$ for $\gamma=2.5$ and
$r=0.7802$ for $\gamma=3.0$. The first is quoted to two figures only: with
$p_d\sim d^{-2.5}$ the mean degree itself converges slowly and the value still
drifts at a cutoff of $d_{\max}=1600$. The independent check is exact rather
than graphical: for uncorrelated Poisson graphs the cover size must reduce to
the Weigt--Hartmann form $1-(2W+W^2)/(2c)$ with $W=\mathcal{W}(c)$, and it does
to ten digits.

\section{Minimum hitting set}
\label{sec:hittingset}

Hitting set --- vertex cover on a hypergraph --- is the natural higher-order
lift of the problem of Ref.~\cite{VW03}. In the hard-core representation
$e^{w}=1-\prod_{i\in e}x_i$, which is $1-x_ix_j$ at cardinality two, and minimum
hitting sets are maximal packings reached as $\mu\to\infty$. Splitting
hyperedges into layers by cardinality so that a node's participation across
cardinalities may be correlated, the fields collapse onto integers and the
replica-symmetric solution is
\begin{align}
  \sigma_m &= \bar\Phi^{(m)}\!\left(1-\sigma_1^{c_1-1},\dots,
                                    1-\sigma_L^{c_L-1}\right),
  \label{eq:hs}\\
  x_c &= 1-\Phi(1-\tau)-\tfrac12\sum_m \ave{k_m}\tau_m\sigma_m,
  \qquad \tau_l=\sigma_l^{c_l-1},\nonumber
\end{align}
with $\bar\Phi^{(m)}$ the inclusion-biased excess of the joint chy-degree
generating function.

The map is order-reversing, and that has a clean consequence. For such an $F$
the composition $G=F\circ F$ is order-preserving, so iterating $G$ from $0$
increases to its least fixed point $a$ and from $1$ decreases to its greatest
$b$, with $F(a)=b$. If $a=b$ there is one fixed point and it is stable; if $a<b$
that pair is a period-two orbit and the fixed point of $F$ lies strictly between
them, unstable. The bracket therefore \emph{finds} the solution and
\emph{diagnoses} it in one object, turning ``the program fails to converge''
into two numbers that say by how much.

Three results follow. First, cardinality alone does nothing: for fixed $c$ and
Poisson hyperdegree the transition is at
\begin{equation}
  \ave{k}_{\mathrm{RSB}}=\frac{e}{c-1},
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \ave{k}(c-1)=e
  \label{eq:kc}
\end{equation}
for every cardinality, verified to twelve digits for $c=2,\dots,20$. Counted in
neighbours, hyperedge size does not move the transition at all, and $c=2$
returns the Bauer--Golinelli value $e$.

Second, \emph{spread} in cardinality postpones it. At fixed mean excess
cardinality $2$, the transition moves from $\ave{k}\bar c=2.72$ for uniform
$c=3$ to $4.84$ for an equal mixture of $c=2$ and $c=4$ and $6.83$ for a
three-to-one mixture of $c=2$ and $c=6$. Heterogeneity keeps the problem easy
longer --- the same direction the degree heterogeneity of Ref.~\cite{VW03}
acts, and the conjunctive structure of a hyperedge constraint does not offset it.

Third, \emph{correlation} across cardinalities is the axis that makes it harder,
and it has no counterpart in an ensemble with one edge type. Anti-correlating a
node's participation in small and large hyperedges at fixed marginals brings the
transition forward monotonically, from $6.83$ to $2.64$.

A confound is worth stating because it is easy to fall into. The obvious
positive-correlation construction --- half the nodes at double the hyperdegree,
half at zero --- returns a transition at exactly half the uncorrelated value.
That is not correlation but dilution: the zero class is isolated vertices, so
the ensemble is the uncorrelated one at half density. Matched constructions keep
both classes populated, and $\Phi(0,\dots,0)$, the fraction of vertices in no
hyperedge, should be reported alongside any such comparison.

\section{Core percolation as a chygraph fixed point}
\label{sec:core}

The order parameter the hyperbolic measurement is about is not the giant
component. Leaf removal deletes a degree-one vertex together with its neighbour
and the residue, every vertex of degree at least two, is the core; it is empty
below a threshold and extensive above, at $c=e$ for Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi
\cite{BG01}. Nothing in the percolation formalism computes it.

It is nonetheless a chygraph fixed point, with a \emph{three-state} message.
Percolation gets away with a scalar because ``is this branch connected'' has two
answers; leaf removal has three. Run the process on the cavity branch at $i$
with complex $a$ deleted and $i$ ends leaf-ready ($L$: surviving with no
surviving neighbour outside $a$), deleted ($D$), or core-side ($C$: surviving
with at least one). Writing $(\lambda,\delta,\gamma)$ for their probabilities,
the chy-degree step is an ordinary generating-function step,
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_m=\bar\Phi^{(m)}(\zeta),\qquad
  \delta_m=1-\bar\Phi^{(m)}(1-\kappa),
  \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
and the intra-complex step is again solved exactly inside the complex. Let $m$
be the number of the complex's other $c-1$ members that are live. Every live
member has clique-degree exactly $m$, so no member is a leaf unless $m=1$, and a
complex can force a node out only through the single configuration in which its
one live member also has no outside edge:
\begin{equation}
  \zeta=\delta^{\,c-1},\qquad \kappa=(c-1)\,\delta^{\,c-2}\lambda.
  \label{eq:zk}
\end{equation}
At $c=2$ these are $\delta$ and $\lambda$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:core} collapses to
$\lambda=\bar G(\delta)$, $\delta=1-\bar G(1-\lambda)$, and the threshold
$\bar G'(1-\lambda)=1$ returns $c=e$ to ten digits. Unlike the hitting-set map
this one is order-\emph{preserving}, so monotone iteration from zero suffices.
Checked against pure leaf removal at $n=4\times10^5$ for cardinalities two,
three and four, the agreement is $10^{-4}$ to $3\times10^{-3}$.

Setting $\gamma=0$ requires $\zeta=1-\kappa$ in every layer, which at $c=2$ is
an identity and at $c\geq3$ forces $\lambda=0$. A chygraph carrying any layer of
cardinality three or more therefore has \emph{no core-free branch}: its core is
strictly positive at every density, where a graph needs $\bar G'(1-\lambda)>1$.

That statement should be read precisely, because the strong version of it is
false. It does not say a complex survives whole. Leaf removal deletes a leaf's
\emph{neighbour}, and that neighbour can be a complex member: a triangle with
one pendant edge on each vertex has an empty core, every vertex removed. What
the algebra gives is only that the core cannot vanish identically, and it can be
arbitrarily small --- with edges at mean chy-degree $1$ and triangles at mean
$10^{-3}$ the core fraction is $1.8\times10^{-4}$. The strong reading holds only
when \emph{every} layer has cardinality at least three, since then no vertex ever
has degree one, leaf removal never fires, and the core is exactly $1-\Phi(0)$.

\subsection{Vertex cover of the induced graph}
\label{sec:cover}

Hitting set and vertex cover sit at the two ends of one family. A hyperedge is
satisfied once one member is taken, so at most $c-1$ may be left out; two members
of a clique are adjacent, so at most \emph{one} may be left out and the uncovered
set is an independent set of the induced graph. The cavity differs in exactly one
place, and it is again inside the complex. Taking $i$ blocks every other member
of every complex containing it, but a complex could only ever have contributed
one member, so the cost of taking $i$ is the \emph{maximum} over the complex and
not the sum,
\begin{equation}
  z_{i\to a}=1-\sum_{b\neq a}\max\Big(0,\max_{j\in b\setminus i} z_{j\to b}\Big),
  \label{eq:vcz}
\end{equation}
giving
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_m=\bar\Phi^{(m)}(\tau),\qquad \tau_l=(1-\sigma_l)^{c_l-1},
  \label{eq:vc}
\end{equation}
against $\tau_l=\sigma_l^{c_l-1}$ and an outer complement in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs}.
Both reduce to the same map at $c=2$, and both are order-reversing.

At $c=2$ the cover size reduces to the Weigt--Hartmann form to machine
precision, and the solution is exact wherever leaf removal certifies it, that is
below $\ave{\kbar}=e$. At $c\geq3$ it is never certified, and the formalism says
so about itself: by the paragraph above, a cardinality-three layer has no
core-free branch, so the leaf-removal core is extensive at every density and no
constructed cover is ever proved minimal. The degeneracy rule inherited from the
graph, which counts a vertex at $z=0$ as half covered, is the visible symptom.
On isolated triangles every vertex sits at $z=0$, the rule returns $1/2$, and
the truth is $2/3$. So the answer to whether this machinery solves vertex cover
is: on graphs and on hypergraphs, yes, with the replica-symmetry-breaking line
computed; on chygraphs of larger cliques it produces a replica-symmetric value
and simultaneously proves that value is not to be trusted.

\section{Field distributions}
\label{sec:population}

Everything so far linearises. Carrying the message as the distribution itself
tests that. Let $P_l(h)$ be the law of the cavity field a node sends up into a
layer-$l$ complex, held as a population of samples; the up step is the
Poisson-compound convolution that replaces the generating-function product, and
the down step is Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} evaluated numerically. Nothing is
linearised, so the result can be read away from the threshold and at any
temperature.

Run at matched neighbour count, six either way, the critical couplings come out
$0.164628$ against the closed-form $0.168236$ for the graph and $0.144343$
against $0.146947$ for the triangle network, errors of $2.1\%$ and $1.8\%$. Both
sit low for the same reason: with a finite number of sweeps the bisection calls
a slowly decaying magnetisation ordered and stops just below the true threshold.
The bias is common to the two systems, so the ratio --- which is the physical
claim --- is an order of magnitude more accurate than either endpoint: a $T_c$
gain of $14.05\%$ against the closed-form $14.49\%$ of Table~\ref{tab:tc}.

A sharper check is available and costs nothing. The derivative of the numerically
emitted field at zero field must equal the $u'$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime}
computed symbolically, two independent enumerations of the same complex. They
agree to $10^{-8}$ for $c=2$ and $c=3$ across $\beta J=0.2$ to $2$, and at $c=2$
the emitted field reproduces $\mathrm{atanh}(\tanh\beta J\tanh h)$ to $10^{-12}$
at every field, not only at zero.

\section{The free energy}
\label{sec:freeenergy}

Equation~(12) of Ref.~\cite{VW03} is a site-plus-link sum with weight $d-1$ per
vertex. It has no chygraph counterpart, because a threshold never needs one.
Anything variational does.

Parameterising the messages as $\nu_{i\to a}(\sigma)\propto e^{h_{i\to a}\sigma}$
and $\hat\nu_{a\to i}(\sigma)\propto e^{u_{a\to i}\sigma}$, the overlap term of
the general Bethe free energy collapses: $h_{i\to a}+u_{a\to i}$ is the
\emph{same} full field $h_i$ for every complex $a$ containing $i$, so each of the
$k_i$ overlap terms equals $\ln Z_i$ and
\begin{equation}
  -\beta f=\sum_l n_l\ave{\ln Z^{(l)}_a}+\ave{(1-k)\ln Z_i},
  \qquad n_l=\frac{\ave{k_l}}{c_l},
  \label{eq:bethe}
\end{equation}
with $Z_a$ the exact partition function \emph{inside} a complex given its
members' cavity fields and $Z_i=2\cosh h_i$. Those weights are not chosen. They
are the M\"obius counting numbers of Sec.~\ref{sec:regions} in the treelike
case, $1$ on every complex and $1-k_v$ on every node: the region construction
supplies the counting and Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} evaluates the free energy with it.

At zero cavity field this closes,
\begin{equation}
  -\beta f_{\mathrm{para}}=\ln 2+\sum_l\frac{\ave{k_l}}{c_l}
    \ln\!\left(\frac{Z^{(l)}_c}{2^{c_l}}\right),
  \label{eq:para}
\end{equation}
with $Z_c$ the partition function of one isolated complex, which for a graph is
the textbook $\ln2+(c/2)\ln\cosh\beta J$. The sampled estimator reproduces it to
machine precision, but only after a step worth recording: split
$\ln Z_i=\ln 2+\ln\cosh h$ and take the first piece exactly rather than
sampling it. Sampling costs a bias of order $\sqrt{\ave{k}/n}\,\ln2$, about
$5\times10^{-3}$ at $\ave{k}=6$ and $n=8\times10^4$, which is larger than the
free-energy differences Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} exists to resolve. The tell was
that the discrepancy was independent of $\beta J$.

With that in place the transition can be located thermodynamically. The gap
between the ordered and paramagnetic branches of $-\beta f$ is zero to machine
precision at $0.8$ and $0.95$ of $\beta J_c$ and lifts off positively above it,
for the graph and for the triangle network alike, agreeing with
Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}. That is a third route to the same number, using
neither the linearisation of Sec.~\ref{sec:stability} nor the order parameter of
Sec.~\ref{sec:population}.

\section{Complexes as regions}
\label{sec:regions}

Every section above treats a node's complexes as independent. That is the Bethe
approximation \emph{on the chygraph}, and it is exact when the chygraph is
locally treelike --- which at the node level means complexes meet in at most one
node. Two cliques sharing an edge are a loop the chygraph cannot see, exactly as
a triangle is a loop an ordinary graph cannot see. Chygraphs move the problem up
one level; they do not remove it.

The region-graph construction is where it goes next. Take the complexes as
regions, close the family under intersection, and assign counting numbers by
M\"obius inversion,
\begin{equation}
  c_R=1-\sum_{R'\supsetneq R}c_{R'},
  \label{eq:mobius}
\end{equation}
so that $\sum_{R\ni v}c_R=1$ for every node. When complexes meet in at most one
node the family closes on the complexes plus the single nodes and a node in $k$
complexes gets $c_v=1-k$: precisely the counting of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe}, so the
Kikuchi construction contains everything computed above and departs from it only
where complexes genuinely overlap. Two triangles on a shared edge separate the
two: M\"obius inversion puts $-1$ on the shared \emph{edge}, the Bethe counting
puts $-1$ on each of its endpoints. Both count nodes correctly; only one
subtracts the correlation.

Table~\ref{tab:overlap} measures what that costs on the graphs this programme is
about. Maximal cliques are taken as complexes and the chy-degree ensemble is
measured from the graph itself, so the chygraph prediction uses no fitted
quantity. The control labelled ``rewired'' carries that identical clique
ensemble arranged treelike, by a bipartite configuration model of nodes and
cliques.

The prediction agrees with the rewired control to $10^{-3}$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:core} is right on its own terms, now on a measured, multi-layer,
non-Poisson ensemble. The remaining discrepancy against the real graph is
therefore clique overlap and nothing else, the ensemble being held fixed. The
chygraph accounts for $65$ to $83\%$ of the core. The degree-matched
configuration model, meanwhile, has no core at all at these densities, predicted
or measured, so the qualitative separation the construction was built to capture
is reproduced; its magnitude is not.

Closing the remainder means passing messages between regions rather than between
complexes. Equation~\eqref{eq:mobius} identifies where the present counting is
wrong; it does not by itself repair the map, and generalised belief propagation
on the region graph is left open.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The chygraph self-consistency map and the Bethe--Peierls cavity recursion are
the same object, and the difference between percolation and statistical
mechanics on a chygraph is the difference between a scalar message and a
measure. Everything above follows from taking that seriously.

Three results are closed-form and cost nothing beyond the existing percolation
machinery. The threshold tensor becomes a stability tensor under an elementwise
reweighting of its moments, so replica-symmetric and de Almeida--Thouless lines
for higher-order structures are available wherever percolation thresholds
already were. A complex solved exactly inside itself transmits more than the
sum of its edges, and the resulting $14.5\%$ shift in $T_c$ at matched mean
degree is invisible to $\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$. Minimum hitting set on a hypergraph
breaks replica symmetry at $\ave{k}(c-1)=e$ for every fixed cardinality, so
hyperedge size does not move the transition when it is counted in neighbours;
cardinality \emph{heterogeneity} postpones it, in the same direction the degree
heterogeneity of Ref.~\cite{VW03} does, while correlation across cardinalities
brings it forward. That last axis has no counterpart in a single-edge-type
ensemble.

Two of these were checked by routes that share no derivation with the first.
The critical temperatures of Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}, obtained by symbolic
linearisation, are reproduced to a few percent by population dynamics on the
full non-linear problem, and the ratio that carries the physical claim to
better than half a percentage point; the free energy of Sec.~\ref{sec:freeenergy}
locates the same transition thermodynamically, using neither the linearisation
nor the order parameter.

What the construction does not do is as definite. Leaf-removal core percolation
is a chygraph fixed point, and on hyperbolic random graphs the chygraph built
from the measured clique ensemble reproduces the qualitative separation from the
degree-matched control --- the control has no core at all at these densities ---
but only $65$--$83\%$ of the core fraction. The deficit is not a failure of the
map: against a rewired control carrying the identical clique ensemble arranged
treelike, the prediction is exact to $10^{-3}$. It is clique \emph{overlap},
the loops that remain once motifs have been promoted to complexes. The region
graph identifies precisely where the Bethe counting goes wrong --- two cliques
sharing an edge want a counting number on the edge, not on its endpoints ---
and closing the gap means passing messages between regions rather than between
complexes, which is left open.

The wider point is that clustering is not one obstruction but a hierarchy of
them. Promoting triangles to complexes removes the triangles from the loop
structure and exposes the loops between triangles. Whether that hierarchy
terminates usefully for a geometric ensemble, where motifs have no
characteristic size, is the question the hyperbolic measurement poses and this
work does not settle. It does settle that the first step is worth taking: the
effects it captures --- a $14.5\%$ shift in $T_c$, a core where the
degree-matched ensemble has none --- are large, and they are exactly the effects
the degree-and-correlation description was built to be unable to see.
