\title{Statistical mechanics on chygraphs}

\author{Alexei V\'azquez}
\affiliation{}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The Bethe--Peierls treatment of statistical-mechanics models on random graphs
with arbitrary degree correlations [V\'azquez and Weigt, Phys.\ Rev.\ E
\textbf{67}, 027101 (2003)] assumes the graph is locally treelike, and so cannot
see clustering. Chygraphs remove that assumption by promoting dense motifs to
complexes. I show that the chygraph percolation map is already a Bethe--Peierls
cavity recursion specialised to a scalar message, and that the extension to
general Hamiltonians is a single substitution: the argument of a generating
function becomes a measure and the product becomes a convolution. Three
consequences follow in closed form. The threshold tensor of the percolation
calculation becomes a stability tensor once its first moments are reweighted by
the mean derivative of the field update, giving the replica-symmetric and de
Almeida--Thouless lines for higher-order structures. A triangle solved exactly
inside itself transmits more per neighbour than an independent edge, so at
matched mean degree clustering raises the Ising critical temperature by
$14.5\%$ --- an effect invisible to the degree-and-correlation ensemble.
Minimum hitting set on a hypergraph has a replica-symmetry-breaking point at
$\ave{k}(c-1)=e$ for every fixed cardinality $c$, and cardinality heterogeneity
postpones it while correlation across cardinalities brings it forward. Finally,
leaf-removal core percolation is a chygraph fixed point with a three-state
message, which quantifies the clustering that a degree-matched configuration
model cannot reproduce.
\end{abstract}

\section{The correspondence}
\label{sec:correspondence}

The cavity recursion of Ref.~\cite{VW03}, Eq.~(4),
\begin{equation}
  h^{(i|j)} = \mu + \sum_{k\neq j} u\!\left(h^{(k|i)}\right),
  \label{eq:vw}
\end{equation}
and the chygraph self-consistency conditions of Ref.~\cite{vazquez2023pre},
\begin{align}
  Q^{ml}_- &= \prod_k \Phi^l_k\!\left(Q^{lk}_-\right)
              \prod_k \Big[\bar G^l_k\Big]_{k=m}\!\!\left(Q^{lk}_+\right),
  \label{eq:Qm}\\
  Q^{ml}_+ &= \prod_k \Big[\bar\Phi^l_k\Big]_{k=m}\!\!\left(Q^{lk}_-\right)
              \prod_k G^l_k\!\left(Q^{lk}_+\right),
  \label{eq:Qp}
\end{align}
are the same object. Both carry a message on a directed link --- an edge in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vw}, an inclusion in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Qm}--\eqref{eq:Qp} --- updated
from every incoming message except the one it came from, the exclusion written
as $k\neq j$ in one case and $[\bar F]_{k=m}$ in the other. Both are exact on a
locally treelike structure and both assume replica symmetry.

Table~\ref{tab:dictionary} gives the correspondence. The one substantive
difference is the message itself: a probability generating function
$\Phi(x)=\sum_n p_n x^n$ evaluated at a scalar becomes, when the argument is a
measure on fields, $\sum_n p_n P^{*n}$, the $n$-fold convolution. That is
Eq.~(10) of Ref.~\cite{VW03} written as a generating function, and it is the
whole content of the extension.

\section{Core percolation as a chygraph fixed point}
\label{sec:core}

% RESULT. Leaf removal needs THREE states, not a scalar: on the cavity branch a
% node ends leaf-ready (L), deleted (D) or core-side (C). Percolation has two
% answers, which is why it gets a scalar.
%
%   lambda_m = Phibar^(m)(zeta),   delta_m = 1 - Phibar^(m)(1 - kappa)
%
% Intra-complex step, exact inside a clique of cardinality c: with m the number
% of live other members, every live member has clique-degree exactly m, so a
% complex can force a node out only at m = 1 with that member having no outside
% edge --
%   zeta = delta^{c-1},   kappa = (c-1) delta^{c-2} lambda.
% c = 2 collapses to lambda = Gbar(delta), delta = 1 - Gbar(1-lambda), threshold
% Gbar'(1-lambda) = 1, i.e. Bauer-Golinelli c = e, recovered to ten digits.
%
% The map is order-PRESERVING, unlike hitting set: monotone iteration from zero.
% Validated against pure leaf removal at n = 4e5 for cardinalities 2, 3, 4,
% errors 1e-4 to 3e-3.
%
% A chygraph with any layer of cardinality >= 3 has no core-free branch, so its
% core is strictly positive at every density while the degree-matched graph has
% none until mean degree e. STATE PRECISELY: this does not mean a complex
% survives whole -- a triangle with a pendant on each vertex has an empty core.
% When EVERY layer has cardinality >= 3 no vertex ever has degree 1, leaf
% removal never fires, and the core is exactly 1 - Phi(0).

\section{Complexes as regions}
\label{sec:regions}

% RESULT (WP5). Everything above treats a node's complexes as independent: the
% Bethe approximation on the chygraph, exact when complexes meet in at most ONE
% node. Two cliques sharing an edge are a loop the chygraph cannot see, just as
% a triangle is a loop a graph cannot see. Chygraphs move the problem up one
% level; they do not remove it.
%
% Region graph: close the complexes under intersection, count by Mobius
% inversion c_R = 1 - sum_{R' > R} c_{R'} so every node is counted once. When
% complexes meet in <= 1 node the family is complexes plus single nodes and
% c_v = 1 - k_v: exactly the Bethe counting. Two triangles on a shared edge:
% Kikuchi puts -1 on the EDGE, Bethe puts -1 on each endpoint. Both count nodes
% correctly; only Kikuchi subtracts the correlation.
