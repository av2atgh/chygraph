\title{Statistical mechanics on chygraphs}

\author{Alexei V\'azquez}

\affiliation{Nodes \& Links Ltd, Salisbury House, Station Road,

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A chygraph is a set of complexes whose vertices are themselves complexes, a
construction that promotes a dense motif to a complex and leaves the structure
over complexes treelike where the underlying graph is not. Percolation on
chygraphs is solved; here I carry the same map to statistical mechanics on
hypergraphs and higher-order structures. The extension is a single
substitution: in the chy-degree step the argument of a generating function
becomes a measure and the product a convolution, while the intra-complex step is
an exact sum over the interior of a complex. Critical points then follow from an
$L\times L$ determinant, $L$ being the number of complex layers, however large
the complexes. Known results --- Molloy--Reed, the Ising and
de Almeida--Thouless lines, Weigt--Hartmann, Bauer--Golinelli, the
regular-hypergraph hitting set of M\'ezard and Tarzia, and the simplicial Ising
model --- are recovered as special cases. Beyond them, four results are new.
Solving a complex exactly inside itself gives its transmission per neighbour in
closed form, $t/(1-t+t^{2})$ for a triangle against $t=\tanh\beta J$ for an
edge; because a traversed complex also removes a branch, the net effect at fixed
degree distribution is that clustering \emph{lowers} the critical temperature,
by $13.9\%$ for two triangles per vertex against the $4$-regular graph, which a
Monte Carlo simulation confirms to $0.3\%$. Minimum hitting set loses its
hard-field solution --- the point at which leaf-removal certification stops ---
at $\ave{k}(c-1)=e$ for every cardinality, while spread in cardinality delays it
and correlation across cardinalities advances it. Leaf-removal core percolation
is a chygraph fixed point with a three-state message, and any layer of
cardinality three or more removes its core-free branch entirely --- which is
exactly the condition under which leaf removal certifies a minimum cover, so
covering and core percolation share one threshold rather than two. On hyperbolic
random graphs the construction gives a core where a degree-matched null model
has none, with a growth exponent of the right size, though it recovers only
$65$--$83\%$ of the magnitude and its predicted exponent shows no dependence on
the degree tail where the measured one does. The limit is equally sharp: a
chygraph is treelike only when its complexes meet in at most one node.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Bethe--Peierls scheme solves a statistical-mechanics model on a random graph
whenever the graph is locally treelike. Reference~\cite{VW03} carried it to
graphs with an arbitrary degree distribution \emph{and} arbitrary correlations
between the degrees of adjacent vertices, and found that such correlations can
change the solution structure of a hard optimisation problem qualitatively:
minimal vertex covers on strongly assortative scale-free graphs sit behind a
replica-symmetry-breaking transition and simple heuristics stop finding them.
The ensemble is specified by two objects, $p_d$ and $e_{dd'}$.

That pair does not encode clustering, and the derivation assumes it away. The
assumption is not innocuous. Hyperbolic random graphs, run through leaf removal,
keep an extensive core at \emph{every} mean degree with no transition at all;
their degree-matched configuration model --- the same $p_d$, the same
assortativity to within a few percent --- is core-free until the mean degree
reaches $4.5$ to $10$. What separates them is the clustering hyperbolic geometry
induces, and it lies outside $\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$ by construction.

The idea of restoring treelikeness by promoting a motif to a super-node is not
new. Newman \cite{New09} and Karrer and Newman \cite{KN10} construct random
graphs with arbitrary distributions of subgraphs and give the message-passing
treatment; Yoon \textit{et al.} \cite{Yoon11} solve the Ising model on precisely
that class of ensembles; and Cantwell and Newman \cite{CN19} reach the same
problems from the other side, by message passing that keeps short loops
explicitly. On the hard-core side Liu \textit{et al.} \cite{Liu12} give the
three-state cavity treatment of leaf-removal core percolation on
configuration-model networks, and Coutinho \textit{et al.} \cite{Cou20} treat
covering problems and core percolation on hypergraphs.

Chygraphs \cite{vazquez2023pre,vazquez2024comnet} carry that construction to
arbitrary depth: a chygraph is a set of complexes whose vertices are themselves
complexes, so graphs, hypergraphs, multiplex hypergraphs and interacting
hypergraphs become one object with different layer data, and percolation on all
of them reduces to one self-consistency map. What is added here is on the model
side rather than the structural side. Reference~\cite{Yoon11} treats the Ising
model with a fixed interior interaction; Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} below leaves the
interior Hamiltonian free, which is what lets one $L\times L$ determinant serve
the Ising model, the simplicial interaction, hitting set and core percolation
without rederivation. Against Ref.~\cite{Liu12} the novelty is not the
three-state message but its extension to complexes of cardinality three or more,
where the core-free branch disappears altogether; against Ref.~\cite{Cou20}, the
soft-field treatment of Sec.~\ref{sec:soft} and the arbitrary chy-degree
distributions it carries.

Bond percolation is the $q\to1$ limit of the random-cluster model, which is the
Fortuin--Kasteleyn representation of the $q$-state Potts model
\cite{FK72}; the percolation map is therefore already a cavity recursion. Site
occupation enters the same map by thinning the generating functions rather than
as a separate limit.
What has to be supplied is the part percolation does not need. Sections~\ref{sec:theory}
and~\ref{sec:structures} give the general theory and its specialisation to
particular structures. Sections~\ref{sec:ising} and~\ref{sec:hs} then work two
models through it, the Ising model and the hitting set problem, general first
and structure by structure after. Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} sets out the
construction, the structures it specialises to, and what distinguishes one
problem from another: not the recursion, but what the message carries. Section~\ref{sec:regions} states the price of
the whole construction: a chygraph is treelike only when its complexes meet in
at most one node, and on a hyperbolic random graph the cliques do not.

\section{General theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Chygraphs and the cavity recursion}

A chygraph is a set of complexes, each a hypergraph whose vertex set lies in the
set of complexes; complex $A$ \emph{includes} $B$ when $B$ is in its vertex set,
an \emph{atom} includes nothing, and layers $l=0,\dots,L-1$ separate complexes
with different statistical properties \cite{vazquez2023pre}. Two matrices of
generating functions specify the ensemble: $\Phi^l_k$ for the number of layer-$k$
complexes that include a layer-$l$ complex, its \emph{chy-degree}, and $G^l_k$
for the layer-$k$ complexes in the intra-complex component of a layer-$l$
complex, with $\bar\Phi$, $\bar G$ the excess counterparts.

A message lives on a directed inclusion and is updated from every incoming
message except the one it came from (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}a). Percolation makes that message a scalar
\cite{vazquez2023pre}; here it is a distribution $P^{ml}_i(h)$ of cavity fields,
and the two steps are
\begin{align}
  \text{up:}\quad & h_{i\to a}=\mu+\sum_{b\ni i,\,b\neq a}u_{b\to i},
  \label{eq:up}\\
  \text{down:}\quad & u_{a\to i}=\text{(field emitted by complex }a\text{)}.
  \label{eq:down}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:up} holds on one realisation. At the level of the ensemble it
becomes a self-consistency condition on the distributions themselves. Let
$Q^{lk}$ be the law of the field a layer-$k$ complex emits onto a layer-$l$
complex, and let a generating function act on a measure by
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^l_k\!\ast\!Q=\sum_{n}p^{lk}_{n}\,Q^{\ast n},
  \qquad \Phi^l_k(x)=\sum_n p^{lk}_n x^n,
  \label{eq:conv}
\end{equation}
the $n$-fold convolution weighted by the chy-degree distribution. The law
$P^{ml}$ of the field a layer-$l$ complex sends up into a layer-$m$ complex is
then the convolution over target layers
\begin{equation}
  P^{ml}=\delta_{\mu}\ast\Big(\ast_{k}\,
    \big[\bar\Phi^{l}_{k}\big]_{k=m}\!\ast\,Q^{lk}\Big),
  \label{eq:ensemble}
\end{equation}
with $\delta_\mu$ the shift by the chemical potential and
$[\bar\Phi]_{k=m}$ the excess generating function on the layer arrived through.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ensemble} is the percolation map of
Ref.~\cite{vazquez2023pre} with one substitution --- the argument of $\Phi$
becomes a measure and the product a convolution --- and it is what the rest of
this paper specialises. Putting $Q^{lk}=\delta_{u}$ for a scalar $u$ collapses
every convolution to a product and returns the percolation map; and since the
first moment of a convolution is the sum of the first moments of its factors,
linearising Eq.~\eqref{eq:ensemble} about the trivial fixed point gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear}.

\subsection{The intra-complex step}
\label{sec:emitted}

Equation~\eqref{eq:down} is where percolation and statistical mechanics part
company. Reachability factorises, so percolation can compress the interior of a
complex into a single component-size generating function; a general interaction
cannot be compressed and must be summed. For a complex holding $c$ members with
internal energy $-\beta H(\sigma)$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    u&=\tfrac12\ln\frac{Z(\sigma_0=+1)}{Z(\sigma_0=-1)},\\
    Z(\sigma_0)&=\!\!\sum_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_{c-1}}\!\!
      e^{-\beta H(\sigma)+\sum_j h_j\sigma_j},
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:emitted}
\end{equation}
exactly, at a cost exponential in $c$ and independent of everything outside.
This is the one place the internal structure of a complex enters, and it is why
promoting a motif to a complex buys anything at all.

\subsection{Linear stability}
\label{sec:stability}

Linearising Eq.~\eqref{eq:up},
\begin{equation}
  \delta h_{i\to a}=\sum_{b\neq a}u'\,\delta h_{b\to i},
  \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
so every branch carries a factor $u'=\partial u/\partial h$ evaluated at the
trivial fixed point --- one number per traversal. The threshold tensor of the
percolation calculation \cite{vazquez2023pre} carries first moments precisely
because there $u'$ is the constant occupation probability, already folded in by
thinning. For any other model the same tensor applies with
\begin{equation}
  \ave{\kappa}\;\longrightarrow\;\ave{\kappa}\ave{u'},
  \label{eq:reweight}
\end{equation}
for the ferromagnetic or replica-symmetric instability; replacing $\ave{u'}$
throughout by $\ave{u'^2}$ gives the de Almeida--Thouless line instead. The reweighting is an elementwise
multiplication: the index structure, the block layout and the determinant are
untouched. The weight sits on the intra-complex channel, since the interaction
lives inside a complex; the chy-degree step is a relay and carries weight one.

Collecting the two steps, arriving at a node through a layer-$l$ complex it
belongs to $\ave{\kbar}_m$ further layer-$m$ complexes when $m=l$ and
$\ave{\kappa}_m$ otherwise, each offering $c_m-1$ other members. The branching
matrix on the $L$ complex layers is therefore
\begin{equation}
  B_{lm}=\Big[\ave{\kbar}_m\delta_{lm}+\ave{\kappa}_m(1-\delta_{lm})\Big]
         (c_m-1)\,u'_m,
  \label{eq:branch}
\end{equation}
and the transition is at $\det(I-B)=0$. The linear algebra is $L\times L$
however large the complexes are: cardinality enters only through $u'_m$.

Two caveats on Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch}. First, $c_m-1$ is the number of other
members seen on arrival, which is that expression only when every complex in
layer $m$ has the same cardinality. For a layer carrying a distribution of
cardinalities the count is size-biased and the correct factor is
$\ave{\sbar}_m=\ave{c^2}_m/\ave{c}_m-1$, the same quantity measured in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ensemble}; $\ave{c}_m-1$ is not equal to it. Throughout this
paper a layer is by construction a single cardinality --- the clique ensembles
of Sec.~\ref{sec:regions} are split into one layer per clique size --- so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} is used with $c_m-1$ and no size-biasing is required.
Second, the correct entry is $\ave{\sbar\,u'}$, a joint average over the
interior of the complex, and writing $\ave{\sbar}\ave{u'}$ assumes $u'$ is
common to the layer, which single-cardinality layers again guarantee.

\subsection{Away from the trivial fixed point}
\label{sec:fixedpoint}

Equation~\eqref{eq:branch} is the Jacobian of the map at its \emph{trivial}
fixed point, and its Perron root is $1+\Lambda$ with $\Lambda$ the threshold
diagnostic of the percolation calculation. The same Jacobian evaluated at the
fixed point the map actually reaches answers a different question --- whether
the solution is stable --- and for percolation the answer is
\begin{equation}
  \rho\big(J(Q^{\ast})\big)=1-\Lambda+O(\Lambda^{2}),
  \label{eq:exchange}
\end{equation}
with the residual $1-\rho-\Lambda$ falling from $-2.9\times10^{-4}$ at
$\Lambda=2.2\times10^{-2}$ to $-7.6\times10^{-7}$ at $\Lambda=1.1\times10^{-3}$.
Divided by $\Lambda^{2}$ it converges, taking the values $-0.599$, $-0.604$,
$-0.609$ and $-0.610$ as $\Lambda$ falls through
$3.9\times10^{-2}$, $2.2\times10^{-2}$, $4.0\times10^{-3}$ and
$1.1\times10^{-3}$, which is what fixes the order at quadratic rather than
merely bounding it at one point. The two roots leave $1$ at the same rate in opposite
directions: an exchange of stability, made quantitative.

One diagnostic does not survive the move, and it is worth naming because it is
the natural thing to reach for. The sign of the leading eigenvalue does
\emph{not} distinguish an order-preserving map from an order-reversing one. The
coupled core of a chygraph alternates between the up and down message roles of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:up}--\eqref{eq:down}, so $J$ is a non-negative matrix of period
two and Perron--Frobenius places eigenvalues at both $+\rho$ and $-\rho$. The
signs of the \emph{entries} are the unambiguous test: non-negative for a map
built from generating functions, non-positive for the hard-core maps of
Sec.~\ref{sec:hs}. That distinction decides which solver applies, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:hs} turns on it.

\subsection{Free energy}
\label{sec:freeenergy}

Parameterising the messages as $\nu_{i\to a}(\sigma)\propto e^{h_{i\to
a}\sigma}$ and $\hat\nu_{a\to i}(\sigma)\propto e^{u_{a\to i}\sigma}$, the
overlap term of the Bethe free energy collapses, because $h_{i\to a}+u_{a\to i}$
is the \emph{same} full field $h_i$ for every complex containing $i$, leaving
one term per complex and $1-k_i$ per node,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    -\beta f&=\sum_l n_l\ave{\ln Z^{(l)}_a}+\ave{(1-k)\ln Z_i},\\
    n_l&=\ave{k_l}/c_l,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:bethe}
\end{equation}
with $Z_a$ the exact partition function inside a complex --- the same sum as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} --- and $Z_i=2\cosh h_i$. At zero cavity field this
closes,
\begin{equation}
  -\beta f_{\mathrm{para}}=\ln 2+\sum_l\frac{\ave{k_l}}{c_l}
    \ln\!\left(Z^{(l)}_c/2^{c_l}\right),
  \label{eq:para}
\end{equation}
$Z_c$ being the partition function of one isolated complex.

\subsection{A note on the numbers quoted}
\label{sec:numbers}

Agreements quoted below to eight, ten or twelve digits are between two
evaluations of the same analytic object --- a closed form against an
enumeration, or a symbolic derivative against a numerical one. They establish
that the implementation is faithful, not that the physics is right. Where a
result is tested against something outside the formalism it is said so
explicitly: the Monte Carlo of Sec.~\ref{sec:ising}, the leaf-removal
simulations of Secs.~\ref{sec:core} and~\ref{sec:regions}, and the published
values of Refs.~\cite{WH00,BG01,MT07,SLG26}.

\subsection{Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

Equation~\eqref{eq:up} treats a node's complexes as independent, which is exact
when the chygraph is locally treelike --- at the node level, when complexes meet
in at most \emph{one} node. Section~\ref{sec:regions} takes that up; until then
it is assumed.

\section{Structures}
\label{sec:structures}

Everything above is fixed once the layers, their cardinalities and the
chy-degree generating function are given (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}b). Table~\ref{tab:structures} lists the
specialisations used below. In each case Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} is written out
and only $u'_m$ remains to be supplied by Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted}.

A graph is one layer of cardinality two, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} collapses to
the scalar $\ave{\kbar}u'=1$. A clique network or a hypergraph of fixed
cardinality is one layer of cardinality $c$, giving $\ave{\kbar}(c-1)u'=1$: the
cardinality enters both as the number of neighbours a complex supplies and
through $u'$, and the two effects are distinct. Two layers of cardinalities two
and three describe a graph with links and triangles,
\begin{equation}
  \ave{\kbar}_L u'_L+2\ave{\kbar}_T u'_T
  +2u'_Lu'_T\!\left(\ave{\kappa}_L\ave{\kappa}_T
                    -\ave{\kbar}_L\ave{\kbar}_T\right)=1,
  \label{eq:LT}
\end{equation}
the last term vanishing whenever the layers are Poisson. A hypergraph with a
spread of cardinalities takes one layer per cardinality, and if a node's
participation across cardinalities is correlated the marginals are replaced by a
joint $\Phi$ and $\ave{\kbar}$ by its inclusion-biased second moment.

\section{The Ising model}
\label{sec:ising}

\subsection{General}

Put an Ising spin $\sigma_i=\pm1$ on every node and couple every pair inside
every complex,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}=-J\sum_{a}\ \sum_{\{i,j\}\subset a}\sigma_i\sigma_j
              \;-\;B\sum_i\sigma_i,
  \label{eq:Hising}
\end{equation}
the first sum running over complexes and the second over nodes, with $B$ an
external field set to zero below. A complex of cardinality $c$ therefore carries
$\binom{c}{2}$ bonds, and a graph is the case $c=2$ where each complex carries
one. Writing $t=\tanh\beta J$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hising} inside it gives $u'$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} gives the
transition; squaring $u'$ gives the de Almeida--Thouless line instead. Nothing else is needed, and the calculation is
$L\times L$ linear algebra with an enumeration of size $2^{c}$ behind each entry.

\subsection{Structure by structure}

For a $c$-clique, Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} yields
\begin{align}
  c=2:&\quad u'=t, \nonumber\\
  c=3:&\quad u'=t/(1-t+t^{2}), \nonumber\\
  c=4:&\quad u'=(t+t^{3})/(1-2t+3t^{2}),
  \label{eq:uprime}
\end{align}
with no useful closed form beyond, but exact by enumeration at any cardinality.
On a graph the transition is therefore $\ave{\kbar}\tanh\beta J=1$ and the de
Almeida--Thouless line $\ave{\kbar}\tanh^2\beta J=1$, both textbook. On a
triangle network it is $2\ave{\kbar}_T\,t/(1-t+t^2)=1$, and on links and
triangles Eq.~\eqref{eq:LT}. Neither line needs a closed form to be evaluated:
at chy-degree $2$ a clique layer gives $\beta J_c=0.5493$, $0.2118$, $0.1304$,
$0.0940$, $0.0734$ for $c=2,\dots,6$, with the de Almeida--Thouless line at
$0.8814$, $0.4024$, $0.2670$, $0.2015$, $0.1625$; the spin-glass instability
always needs the stronger coupling, since $u'^{2}<u'$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:uprime} gives $t/(1-t+t^2)>t$ on $0<t<1$, reducing to $t$ at
leading order: \emph{per traversal}, a triangle transmits more than an
independent edge. It does not follow that clustering favours order, and it does
not. Equation~\eqref{eq:branch} carries a multiplicity as well as a
transmission, and the two move in opposite directions. Arriving at a
degree-$n$ vertex of a graph, $n-1$ further neighbours branch. Arriving at the
same vertex through one of its $n/2$ triangles, only $n-2$ branch, because one
of the two neighbours in the triangle just traversed is already accounted for
and its influence sits inside $u'$ rather than being counted again. At $n=4$
the condition is $3t=1$ for links against $2u'_T=1$ for triangles, so the
enhancement would have to carry $u'_T$ from $1/3$ to $1/2$ to break even, and
it reaches only $3/7$. The lost branch wins.

Table~\ref{tab:tc} states the comparison against two null models. The
unambiguous one is regular: an $n$-regular graph against a network of $n/2$
triangles per vertex. These have identical degree distribution
$p_d=\delta_{d,n}$, identically neutral $e_{dd'}$, and the same number of edges
per vertex; they differ only in whether those edges close into triangles.
Clustering \emph{lowers} $T_c$, by $13.9\%$ at four neighbours and $0.3\%$ at
twenty, the effect being $O(t^2)$ and so decaying as $1/\ave{k}$. This is the
sign familiar from percolation, where clustering raises the threshold at fixed
degree sequence, and it arises here by the same redundancy.

The Poisson comparison requires care and is included because it is the one that
misleads. Taking a layer of $n$ links against a layer of $n/2$ triangles, both
Poisson, matches the \emph{mean} degree and nothing else: the triangle
construction gives $d=2X$ with $X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(n/2)$, so the degree is
even, its variance is $2n$ rather than $n$, and the excess degree that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} actually uses is $\ave{\kbar}=n+1$ rather than $n$. Read
against a configuration model carrying that same excess degree, the sign is the
same as in the regular case.

What is not visible to $\{p_d,e_{dd'}\}$ is therefore the \emph{magnitude} of
the shift, not its existence: no configuration model on the same degree
distribution reproduces the triangle ensemble's $T_c$, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime} supplies the difference in closed form. The two ensembles
themselves are of course distinguishable --- by their clustering coefficient, if
by nothing else.

\subsection{Checks}

Three calculations test Eqs.~\eqref{eq:branch} and~\eqref{eq:uprime}, and one
tests the physics. The distinction matters and is drawn explicitly, because the
first three share the paper's own equations and cannot by themselves establish
that the ensemble comparison is the right one.

Carrying the message as a distribution and iterating a population of samples ---
the up step a Poisson-compound convolution, the down step Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted}
evaluated numerically --- gives $\beta J_c=0.164628$ against the closed-form
$0.168236$ for a graph at six neighbours and $0.144343$ against $0.146947$ for
the corresponding triangle network, errors of $2.1\%$ and $1.8\%$, both low
because a finite sweep budget calls a slowly decaying magnetisation ordered.
Independently, the gap between the ordered and paramagnetic branches of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} is zero to machine precision below the transition and lifts
off above it, at the same place. And the derivative of the numerically emitted
field agrees with Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime} to $10^{-8}$ for $c=2$ and $c=3$. All
three are implementation checks: two evaluations of the same analytic object,
or two solutions of the same fixed point.

The physical test is a direct simulation, which uses no cavity equation.
Wolff cluster updates on a $4$-regular random graph and on a network of two
triangles per vertex --- the pair of Table~\ref{tab:tc}, every vertex at degree
exactly $4$ in both --- give the Binder cumulant crossings
\begin{equation}
  T_c^{\mathrm{links}}=2.894,\qquad T_c^{\mathrm{tri}}=2.482,
  \label{eq:mc}
\end{equation}
at $n=3000$ and $12000$, against $2.8854$ and $2.4853$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch}: agreement to $0.29\%$ and $0.14\%$, and a shift of
$-14.2\%$ against a predicted $-13.9\%$. Both the size and the sign of the
clustering effect are therefore confirmed outside the formalism.

\subsection{A different interaction inside the complex}
\label{sec:simplicial}

Nothing in Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} fixed what is inside a complex. Equation
\eqref{eq:emitted} sums whatever Hamiltonian is put there and the chy-degree
step never sees it, so a different higher-order interaction is a substitution
rather than a new theory. The simplicial Ising model of Son, Lee and Goh
\cite{SLG26} is the test case. Where Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hising} couples every pair
inside a complex, here a hyperedge lowers the energy only when all its members
agree,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}=-\sum_e J_{|e|}\,\delta_{\{\sigma_i\}_{i\in e}}-B\sum_i\sigma_i,
  \label{eq:sim}
\end{equation}
with $\delta$ equal to one on the two unanimous states of a hyperedge and zero
on the other $2^q-2$, and the same spins $\sigma_i=\pm1$ and external field $B$
as Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hising}. Their Bethe--Peierls equation is Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sim} substituted, on a chygraph of two layers with fixed
chy-degree.

The sum closes. With $a=\beta J_q$ and the other members at cavity field $h$,
\begin{equation}
  Z(\sigma_0=\pm1)=(2\cosh h)^{q-1}+(e^{a}-1)\,e^{\pm h(q-1)},
  \label{eq:simZ}
\end{equation}
so the emitted field is elementary at any cardinality, where the all-pairs
clique of Sec.~\ref{sec:ising} needs an enumeration, and
\begin{equation}
  u'=\frac{e^{a}-1}{2^{q-1}+e^{a}-1},
  \label{eq:simu}
\end{equation}
which is $\tanh(\beta J/2)$ at $q=2$: a simplicial pair is an ordinary bond of
half the coupling, since $\delta_{\sigma_0\sigma_1}=(1+\sigma_0\sigma_1)/2$. Equation
\eqref{eq:simu} is the derivative with respect to \emph{one} other member's
field, the same convention as Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime}, so the multiplicity $q-1$
is supplied by Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} and not here. Differentiating instead with
respect to a field common to all $q-1$ others multiplies Eq.~\eqref{eq:simu} by
$q-1$; that combination is what enters a symmetric fixed point directly, and
using it in Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} would count the multiplicity twice.

\emph{One caveat, and it is the substantive one.} The branching matrix
\eqref{eq:branch} locates a linear instability of the paramagnet. Where the
transition is discontinuous --- which for this model is most of the interesting
range --- that is the spinodal and not the transition. The ordered branch must
be found by iterating from a magnetised start, and the true temperature by
comparing free energies as in Sec.~\ref{sec:ising}. Equation~\eqref{eq:branch}
is still the right object; it answers a different question.

Read that way, the construction reproduces their results and extends them.
Normalisation has to be stated first, because the two treatments fix different
things. At fixed coupling $J$ and chy-degree $4$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:simu} with
$3(q-1)u'=1$ gives $e^{a}=2$ at $q=2$ and again at $q=4$, hence the same
$T=1/\ln2=1.4427$, and $e^{a}=9/5$ at $q=3$, hence $T=1.7013$: the spinodal is
non-monotonic in cardinality and peaks at $q=3$. Reference~\cite{SLG26} instead
fixes $\rho_qJ_q=1$ with $\rho_q$ the density of hyperedges, which for $k$
complexes of size $q$ at every vertex means $J_q=q/k$. Imposing that
normalisation on Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch} and letting the chy-degree grow,
\begin{equation}
  T^{\ast}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{q(q-1)}{2^{\,q-1}}
  \qquad (k\to\infty),
  \label{eq:BWlimit}
\end{equation}
which is their Eq.~(8). The residual falls as $1/k$: at $q=3$ it is
$1.5\times10^{-2}$, $3.8\times10^{-3}$ and $9.4\times10^{-4}$ for $k=50$, $200$
and $800$, halving with each doubling of the chy-degree, and the same at
$q=2,5,6,8$. The exception is $q=4$, where the residual falls as $1/k^{2}$ and
reaches $1.4\times10^{-6}$ by $k=800$: the leading finite-connectivity
correction vanishes at the cardinality which is tricritical in the mean-field
theory, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:BWlimit} is reached faster there than anywhere else. Its maximum of $3/2$ is shared by $q=3$ and $q=4$, as they
report; at finite chy-degree the degeneracy is lifted. The non-monotonicity is
therefore common to the two treatments and the location of its maximum is not,
which is a statement about connectivity rather than a disagreement. At chy-degree $4$ the transition
is continuous through $q=5$ and discontinuous from $q=6$, where Bragg--Williams
places the tricritical cardinality at four. The two do not disagree. Repeating
the calculation as a function of chy-degree, the boundary sits between five and
six only at $k=3$ and $k=4$ and has moved to between four and five by $k=6$,
where it stays out to $k=400$ --- the Bragg--Williams value, recovered as the
connectivity grows. Low connectivity delays the discontinuity by one in
cardinality, and that is a finite-connectivity effect in the regime an
infinite-range theory cannot reach rather than a correction to it.

Their Fig.~4, a double transition at $q=16$ with
$(J_q,J_2,k_q,k_2)=(0.3,0.7,4,4)$, comes out in full, and it is worth doing
properly rather than by hysteresis alone. The continuous transition sits at
$T=1.0108$, which is the pairwise value $3\tanh(J_2/2T)=1$ to three digits
because $u'$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:simu} is $O(2^{-q})$ at $q=16$ and the
higher-order layer contributes nothing to the linear instability. Between the
two transitions the pairwise share of the magnetisation exceeds the
higher-order share by two to three orders of magnitude.

The second transition is genuinely first order, and locating it needs the free
energy. The two branches are obtained by iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft}'s
analogue for this model, $u_l=\,$Eq.~\eqref{eq:simZ} evaluated at
$h-u_l$ with $h=\sum_l k_l u_l$, from a magnetised start and from a
near-zero one; they differ on $0.9770<T<0.9810$, the lower limit being the
spinodal of the weakly ordered branch and the upper the point where the
strongly ordered one ceases to exist. Outside that window the two starts
converge on the same fixed point and the free-energy difference is rounding
noise, so the comparison has to be bracketed inside it. Comparing Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} between
them --- with $Z_i=2\cosh h$ and
$Z_a=(2\cosh h_c)^q+(e^{a}-1)\,2\cosh(qh_c)$, $h_c=h-u_l$ --- the branches cross
at
\begin{equation}
  T_{c}=0.9790,\qquad \Delta m=0.286,
  \label{eq:simTc}
\end{equation}
with $m$ jumping from $0.548$ to $0.834$, and $T^\ast<T_c<T^{\ast\ast}$ as it
must. Below it the two shares of the magnetisation become comparable, their
ratio falling from several hundred to about three, and both jump, the
higher-order one by a factor of forty. Each of those is a claim of
Ref.~\cite{SLG26}, recovered without changing anything outside
Eq.~\eqref{eq:simZ}.

Figure~\ref{fig:simplicial} shows both transitions and the comparison that
locates the second: the two branches of the order parameter with the
coexistence window between them, and the free-energy difference crossing zero
inside it.

$T_c=0.938$, $0.714$ and $0.480$ for $q=6$, $8$ and $12$, each strictly inside
its metastability limits and each with $\Delta m>0.9$; at $q=3$ no coexistence
window exists at all and the transition is continuous, as it should be. Two
practical points. The spinodal is the \emph{lower} limit of metastability and
not the transition, so quoting it as $T_c$ would be wrong by $10$--$50\%$ here.
And just below a continuous transition the near-zero branch converges slowly
enough that a finite iteration budget can mimic coexistence; that is critical
slowing down, and a coexistence test has to be kept away from it.

\section{The hitting set problem}
\label{sec:hs}

\subsection{General}

A hitting set meets every hyperedge; minimising it generalises vertex cover to
hypergraphs \cite{MT07}. Put $x_i=1$ when node $i$ is \emph{in} the set and
$x_i=0$ when it is not, throughout this section, and take
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}=\mu\sum_i x_i
  \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
  \prod_{e}\Big[1-\prod_{i\in e}(1-x_i)\Big]=1,
  \label{eq:Hhs}
\end{equation}
the constraint forbidding a hyperedge none of whose members is taken and the
chemical potential $\mu$ penalising every member of the set, so that minimum
hitting sets are the ground states reached as $\mu\to\infty$. It is often
convenient to work with the complement $y_i=1-x_i$, which is a packing --- no
hyperedge lies wholly inside it --- and the hard-field limit below is written
that way, the cover being one minus the packing density.

In that limit the fields collapse onto integers, $h=\mu z$, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:up} becomes
\begin{equation}
  z_i=1-\#\big\{\,b\ni i:\ z_{j\to b}=1\ \ \forall\,j\in b\setminus i\,\big\},
  \label{eq:zhs}
\end{equation}
so with $\sigma_l$ the chance a node reached through a layer-$l$ complex has
$z=1$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \sigma_m&=\bar\Phi^{(m)}(1-\tau),\\
    \tau_l&=\sigma_l^{\,c_l-1}.
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:hs}
\end{equation}
The map is order-\emph{reversing}: raising any message lowers every message.
Plain iteration therefore oscillates and, past the point where the map loses
stability, never settles --- which is how Ref.~\cite{VW03} detects that point.
It need not be. For an $F$ continuous and order-reversing on $[0,1]^{L}$ ---
both hold for Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs}, since $\bar\Phi^{(m)}$ is a power series with
non-negative coefficients and $\sigma\mapsto1-\sigma^{c-1}$ is decreasing ---
the composition $F\circ F$ is continuous and order-preserving, so iterating it
from $0$ and from $1$ returns its least and greatest fixed points $a\leq b$ with
$F(a)=b$; $a=b$ is a single stable fixed
point and $a<b$ is a period-two orbit with the fixed point of $F$ strictly
between them, unstable. The bracket finds the solution and diagnoses it at once.

\subsection{Structure by structure}

At cardinality two Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs} is the vertex cover problem of
Ref.~\cite{VW03}, and for uncorrelated Poisson graphs the cover size reduces to
the Weigt--Hartmann form \cite{WH00} $1-(2W+W^2)/(2c)$ with $W=\mathcal{W}(c)$,
reproduced to ten digits. With the degree correlations $e_{dd'}=q_d[r\delta_{dd'}+(1-r)
q_{d'}]$ of Ref.~\cite{VW03} the map closes on a single scalar and the bracket
above locates the loss of stability at $r=0.70$ for $\gamma=2.5$ and $r=0.7802$
for $\gamma=3.0$; the first is quoted to two figures because with
$p_d\sim d^{-2.5}$ the mean degree itself converges slowly.

At fixed cardinality $c$ with Poisson chy-degree the excess generating function
is $\bar\Phi(x)=e^{\ave{k}(x-1)}$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs} closes on one scalar,
$\sigma=e^{-\ave{k}\sigma^{c-1}}$. Differentiating,
$|F'(\sigma)|=\ave{k}(c-1)\sigma^{c-2}\,e^{-\ave{k}\sigma^{c-1}}
=\ave{k}(c-1)\sigma^{c-1}$, so with $y=\ave{k}\sigma^{c-1}$ the fixed point is
$\sigma=e^{-y}$, $y=\ave{k}e^{-(c-1)y}$ and the instability $|F'|=1$ is
$(c-1)y=1$. Eliminating $y=1/(c-1)$ gives
$\ave{k}=y\,e^{(c-1)y}=e/(c-1)$, that is
\begin{equation}
  \ave{k}(c-1)=e,
  \label{eq:kc}
\end{equation}
verified to twelve digits for $c=2,\dots,20$. Counted in \emph{neighbours},
hyperedge size does not move the point at all, and $c=2$ returns the
Bauer--Golinelli value $e$ \cite{BG01}.

What Eq.~\eqref{eq:kc} locates is the loss of stability of the \emph{hard-field}
map, which is where leaf-removal-style certification stops. At $c=2$ that
coincides with the accepted replica-symmetry-breaking point, which is why $e$
comes out; above $c=2$ it does not, for the reason given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:hardfield}, and Sec.~\ref{sec:soft} gives the criterion that does.
Read in those terms, two effects are worth recording. The mixed-cardinality numbers follow from the same map with more than one layer.
For layers of cardinality $c_l$ carrying Poisson chy-degrees $\ave{k_l}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs} becomes
$\sigma=\exp\!\big[-\sum_l\ave{k_l}\sigma^{c_l-1}\big]$, one scalar again
because Poisson layers share an excess generating function, and the instability
condition is $\sum_l\ave{k_l}(c_l-1)\sigma^{c_l-1}=1$. Solving the pair for the
three-to-one mixture of $c=2$ and $c=6$, for instance, gives $\sigma=0.3777$ and
$\ave{k}=3.415$, hence the $6.83$ quoted below. Spread in cardinality
moves the point upward. The quantity held fixed is the mean number of
\emph{neighbours} a complex supplies, $\nu=\sum_l\ave{k_l}(c_l-1)/\sum_l\ave{k_l}$,
set to $2$; the quantity reported is $\ave{k}\nu$, the mean number of neighbours
per node. It runs from $2.72$ for uniform $c=3$ to $4.84$ for an equal mixture
of $c=2$ and $c=4$ and $6.83$ for a three-to-one mixture of $c=2$ and $c=6$, so
heterogeneity keeps certification working longer --- the same direction the
degree heterogeneity of Ref.~\cite{VW03} acts. Correlation across cardinalities
acts the other way: anti-correlating a node's participation in small and large
hyperedges at fixed marginals brings the point forward monotonically, from
$6.83$ to $2.64$. That axis has no counterpart in an ensemble with one edge
type.

A confound is worth naming. The obvious positive-correlation construction, half
the nodes at double the chy-degree and half at zero, returns a point at exactly
half the uncorrelated value; that is not correlation but dilution, the zero class
being isolated vertices. Matched constructions keep both classes populated, and
$\Phi(0,\dots,0)$ should be reported alongside any such comparison.

\subsection{Vertex cover of the induced graph}
\label{sec:cover}

Hitting set and vertex cover sit at two ends of one family. Both minimise
$\mathcal{H}=\mu\sum_i x_i$ and differ only in the constraint: where
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hhs} forbids a hyperedge with no member taken, vertex cover of the
induced graph forbids an \emph{edge} with neither endpoint taken,
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{a}\ \prod_{\{i,j\}\subset a}\Big[1-(1-x_i)(1-x_j)\Big]=1,
  \label{eq:Hvc}
\end{equation}
so a hyperedge is satisfied once one member is taken and $c-1$ may be left out,
while two members of a clique are adjacent and at most \emph{one} may. The cavity differs in one place,
again inside the complex: taking $i$ blocks every other member of every complex
containing it, but a complex could only ever have contributed one member, so the
cost is the \emph{maximum} over the complex and not the sum,
\begin{equation}
  z_{i\to a}=1-\sum_{b\neq a}\max\Big(0,\max_{j\in b\setminus i}z_{j\to b}\Big),
  \label{eq:vcz}
\end{equation}
giving
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \sigma_m&=\bar\Phi^{(m)}(\tau),\\
    \tau_l&=(1-\sigma_l)^{c_l-1},
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:vc}
\end{equation}
against $\tau_l=\sigma_l^{c_l-1}$ and an outer complement in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs}.
Both collapse to the same map at $c=2$, and both are order-reversing. Both are
also hard-field maps, so Sec.~\ref{sec:hardfield} applies to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vcz} unchanged: the induced-graph cover size is exact at
cardinality two and an approximation above it, and on isolated triangles, where
every vertex sits at $z=0$, it returns $1/2$ against a true $2/3$.

\subsection{What the hard-field ansatz misses}
\label{sec:hardfield}

Equation~\eqref{eq:zhs} is warning propagation: the $\mu\to\infty$ limit of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hhs} taken with \emph{hard} fields, scaling $h=\mu z$ and keeping
only the integer $z$.
That discards an $O(1)$ contribution which survives the limit. At cardinality
two the discard is harmless. Above it, it is not, and two failures show it.

First the cover size. A vertex at $z=0$ is degenerate and Ref.~\cite{VW03}
counts it as half covered, right when the degeneracy is two-fold. Take disjoint
$3$-hyperedges, one per vertex: there is no interaction between complexes at
all, replica symmetry is trivially valid, one of every three vertices must be
taken, the truth is $1/3$ --- and the rule returns $1/2$. Second, the stability
point of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kc} is a property of the hard-field map and not of the
full replica-symmetric solution; the two coincide only at cardinality two, which
is why $c=2$ returns $e$. On random regular hypergraphs the hard-field criterion
reports broken symmetry across almost the whole $(L,K)$ grid where Fig.~4 of
Ref.~\cite{MT07} finds a genuine replica-symmetric region, though it does
reproduce their result that cardinality two breaks for every degree.

\subsection{Keeping the fields}
\label{sec:soft}

Both failures are repaired by not scaling the fields at all. Working directly
with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hhs} --- $x_i=1$ for a vertex in the set, weight
$e^{-\mu\sum_ix_i}$, no change of variable to the packing --- belief propagation
on the chygraph reads
\begin{align}
  v_{a\to i}&=-\ln\Big[1-\prod_{j\in a\setminus i}\big(1+e^{h_{j\to a}}\big)^{-1}\Big],
  \nonumber\\
  h_{i\to a}&=-\mu+\sum_{b\ni i,\,b\neq a}v_{b\to i},
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{align}
with $\rho=\sigma\big(-\mu+\sum_{b\ni i}v_{b\to i}\big)$. The chy-degree step is
again the convolution of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}; the complex step is again a sum
over the interior, here over the single forbidden configuration in which no
member is taken. Nothing is scaled by $\mu$, so the $O(1)$ parts survive and
$\rho$ emerges as a genuine $O(1)$ number rather than a count of integers.

For a regular hypergraph, $L$ complexes per node of cardinality $K$, symmetry
closes Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    h_{\mathrm{RS}}&=-\frac{\mu}{L}-\frac{L-1}{L}\ln(K-1),\\
    \rho&=1/K,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:hRS}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~(11) of Ref.~\cite{MT07}, reproduced here to four decimals by
population dynamics. Note that $h$ carries a fraction of $\mu$, not an integer
multiple: that is precisely the structure the hard-field ansatz cannot
represent. The full field is $O(1)$, $H=-\ln(K-1)$, and $\rho=1/K$ says the
counting bound is saturated --- one member of every complex and no waste.

Table~\ref{tab:soft} collects the repair. Disjoint $3$-hyperedges return $1/3$;
the graph at $K=2$ returns the Weigt--Hartmann \cite{WH00} cover size, since hard
and soft fields agree there; and the correction grows with the weight the
ensemble puts on complexes of cardinality three or more, reaching $56\%$ for
$c=4$ alone and falling below $1\%$ once ordinary edges dominate.

Equation~\eqref{eq:soft} also carries its own validity criterion, which is what
replaces Eq.~\eqref{eq:kc} above cardinality two. The Bethe free energy of
Sec.~\ref{sec:freeenergy} applies with
$Z_i=1+e^{H_i}$ and $Z_a=\prod_{i\in a}(1+e^{h_{i\to a}})-1$, the complex term
being the sum over its interior with the one forbidden configuration removed, so
\begin{equation}
  s=\sum_l n_l\ave{\ln Z_a}+\ave{(1-k)\ln Z_i}+\mu\rho .
  \label{eq:sgen}
\end{equation}
For a regular hypergraph this reduces to
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    s(\mu\to\infty)&=\frac{1}{K}\Big[a\ln(K-1)-(a-1)\ln K\Big],\\
    a&=(L-1)(K-1),
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~(13) of Ref.~\cite{MT07} and is reproduced by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sgen} to nine digits. Where $s$ turns negative the answer is
unphysical and one-step replica symmetry breaking is required.

Equation~\eqref{eq:sgen} holds for an arbitrary chygraph, with two practical
qualifications. It is not numerically cheap: $\sum_l n_l\ave{\ln Z_a}$ and
$\mu\rho$ are each $O(\mu)$ while $s$ is $O(1)$, so a heterogeneous ensemble
needs both to a relative precision of order $s/\mu$, and only time-averaging the
estimator brings that within reach. The regular case is exact because symmetry
removes the sampling entirely. And the criterion is sufficient but not
necessary: a negative $s$ proves the replica-symmetric answer wrong, a positive
one does not prove it right, since the solution can be unstable while its
entropy is still positive, the general cavity situation of Ref.~\cite{MP01}. Vertex cover on Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graphs is the
example --- replica symmetry breaks at mean degree $e$, yet
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sgen} gives $s=+0.093(8)$ at mean degree $1$ and a value still
positive at $3$. Reference~\cite{MT07} pairs the entropy with a separate
stability criterion for exactly this reason. At $L=4$, $K=6$ the entropy is $-0.157$ and the
replica-symmetric $1/6$ sits below the $0.178$ of Ref.~\cite{MT07}, as an
underestimate should; the hard-field $0.252$ sits above it and further away. At
$L=6$, $K=12$ the entropy is $-0.192$ and the iteration does not settle at all,
which is the visible form of the same failure. Equation~\eqref{eq:soft} also
inherits the treelike assumption of Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}, unchanged.

What it adds beyond Ref.~\cite{MT07} is the chygraph structure. Equation
\eqref{eq:soft} carries an arbitrary chy-degree distribution, mixed
cardinalities and correlated layers, none of which the regular ansatz reaches,
and the last four rows of Table~\ref{tab:soft} are outside it.

\section{Core percolation}
\label{sec:core}

This section carries no new Hamiltonian, and that is worth stating precisely
rather than leaving as an omission. Core percolation is defined by an algorithm,
but the algorithm is the one that solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hvc}: leaf removal takes
a degree-one vertex, puts its neighbour in the cover, and deletes both, which is
an exact step for minimum vertex cover because some minimum cover contains that
neighbour. The core is what the algorithm cannot decide. So core percolation is
not a fifth model but the obstruction to solving the fourth, and
Refs.~\cite{Liu12,Cou20} treat the two together for the same reason.

The two are the same transition, not merely related ones. On a graph,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kc} places the loss of stability of the hard-field map at
$\ave{k}(c-1)=e$, hence at $\ave{k}=e$; the core-percolation threshold below is
the Bauer--Golinelli value $\ave{k}=e$ \cite{BG01}; and these agree to nine
digits because they are one point computed twice. Above cardinality two the
core-free branch disappears altogether, which is exactly why the certification
invoked in Sec.~\ref{sec:hardfield} fails there --- the two statements are the
same statement.

Leaf removal deletes a degree-one vertex with its neighbour and the residue is
the core, empty below a threshold and extensive above, at $c=e$ for
Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi \cite{BG01}. It is a chygraph fixed point, with a
\emph{three}-state message: run the process on the cavity branch and a node ends
leaf-ready ($L$), deleted ($D$), or core-side ($C$). Percolation has two answers
and gets a scalar; this has three. Writing $(\lambda,\delta,\gamma)$ for their
probabilities,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \lambda_m&=\bar\Phi^{(m)}(\zeta),\\
    \delta_m&=1-\bar\Phi^{(m)}(1-\kappa),
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
and the intra-complex step is solved exactly as in Sec.~\ref{sec:emitted}. With
$m$ the number of a complex's other $c-1$ members still live, every live member
has clique-degree exactly $m$, so nothing is a leaf unless $m=1$ and a complex
can force a node out only when its single live member also has no outside edge:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \zeta&=\delta^{\,c-1},\\
    \kappa&=(c-1)\,\delta^{\,c-2}\lambda.
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:zk}
\end{equation}
At $c=2$ this is $\lambda=\bar G(\delta)$, $\delta=1-\bar G(1-\lambda)$ with
threshold $\bar G'(1-\lambda)=1$, returning $c=e$ to ten digits. Unlike
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs} this map is order-\emph{preserving}, so monotone iteration
from zero suffices. Against pure leaf removal at $n=4\times10^5$ for
cardinalities two, three and four the agreement is $10^{-4}$ to
$3\times10^{-3}$.

Setting $\gamma=0$ requires $\zeta=1-\kappa$ in every layer, an identity at
$c=2$ and forcing $\lambda=0$ at $c\geq3$. A chygraph carrying any layer of
cardinality three or more therefore has no core-free branch, so leaf removal
never certifies a cover there. That is the statement Sec.~\ref{sec:hardfield}
uses, and it is now visible as a property of Eq.~\eqref{eq:zk} rather than an
assumption imported from elsewhere: the cover size of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs} can be
believed exactly when Eq.~\eqref{eq:core} admits $\gamma=0$, and both hold only
at cardinality two. The strong reading of that is false and worth blocking: it does \emph{not}
say a complex survives whole, since leaf removal deletes a leaf's
\emph{neighbour} and a triangle with one pendant edge per vertex has an empty
core. What the algebra gives is only that the core cannot vanish identically,
and it can be $1.8\times10^{-4}$.

\section{Overlapping complexes}
\label{sec:regions}

Section~\ref{sec:validity} assumed complexes meet in at most one node. Two
cliques sharing an edge are a loop the chygraph cannot see, exactly as a triangle
is a loop an ordinary graph cannot see: chygraphs move the problem up one level
and do not remove it.

Closing the complexes under intersection and assigning counting numbers by
Möbius inversion,
\begin{equation}
  c_R=1-\sum_{R'\supsetneq R}c_{R'},
  \label{eq:mobius}
\end{equation}
makes every node counted once. When complexes meet in at most one node the
family closes on the complexes plus the single nodes and $c_v=1-k$: precisely the
counting of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe}, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:mobius} contains everything
above and departs from it only where complexes overlap. Two triangles on a shared
edge separate the two --- Möbius inversion puts $-1$ on the shared \emph{edge},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} puts $-1$ on each endpoint. Both count nodes correctly; only
one subtracts the correlation.

That example is small enough to settle by enumeration, which is worth doing
rather than asserting. Four spins, all pairs coupled inside each triangle, with
$\ln Z$ evaluated exactly and under the two counting schemes, gives
Table~\ref{tab:kikuchi}. The correction is a factor of three to eighty, and the
two schemes err in opposite directions: Eq.~\eqref{eq:bethe} overestimates
$\ln Z$ because it subtracts the two shared vertices independently and so never
removes the correlation between them, while Eq.~\eqref{eq:mobius} removes it and
slightly overshoots. That is the size of what the present treatment leaves on
the table.

sharper test would need an ensemble in which the predicted exponent is made to
move, and an analytic account of what fixes it near $1.57$ in

\subsection{What is left}

At $\tau=2.1$ the chygraph \emph{over}-predicts, by up
to a third, which is consistent with the clique ensemble having no
$n$-independent second moment below $\tau=2.5$: the measured ensemble is not
converged there and neither is anything built on it. Closing the remaining
deficit means passing messages between regions rather than between complexes;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mobius} says where the present counting is wrong without repairing
the map. That repair is generalised belief propagation \cite{YFW05}, for which
Ref.~\cite{CN19} is the natural point of comparison.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:code}

Everything above is one object. A chygraph is fixed by its complex layers, their
cardinalities and the chy-degree distribution, and a single class holds that
structure while each calculation in this paper is one of its methods. The
mapping from every numbered equation to the method that evaluates it is given in
the Supplemental Material, and the code is available at the repository cited in
the acknowledgments.

Three points about the arrangement carry physical content rather than
bookkeeping. First, the intra-complex sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted} takes the
interior Hamiltonian as an argument rather than hard-coding it, which is what
made Sec.~\ref{sec:simplicial} a substitution rather than a rederivation.
Second, the hard-field and soft-field hitting-set solvers are kept separate
rather than unified behind one interface, because they answer different
questions and agree only at cardinality two; the routine that reports whether
the hard-field cover size can be believed returns true exactly when every
cardinality is two, which by Sec.~\ref{sec:core} is exactly when the
leaf-removal core has a core-free branch. Third, the routine that returns a
critical coupling refuses the simplicial interaction and directs the caller to
the spinodal instead, because for that model the transition is usually
discontinuous and returning a spinodal under the name of a critical coupling
would be wrong by ten to fifty per cent --- as Sec.~\ref{sec:simplicial} shows.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Chygraphs carry the Bethe--Peierls scheme to structures the degree-and-correlation
ensemble cannot describe, and the whole of the extension is one substitution in
the chy-degree step together with an exact sum inside each complex,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:emitted}. What follows is $L\times L$ linear algebra in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch}, however large the complexes are.

For the Ising model this is complete. Critical temperatures and de
Almeida--Thouless lines follow for any chygraph whose complexes can be
enumerated. The result they deliver is that a complex transmits more per
neighbour than an edge, $t/(1-t+t^2)$ against $t$ for a triangle, but also
removes a branch from Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch}, and the second effect is the
larger: at fixed degree distribution clustering \emph{lowers} $T_c$, by $13.9\%$
for two triangles per vertex against the $4$-regular graph. Monte Carlo puts the
shift at $-14.2\%$. What no configuration model on the same degree distribution
reproduces is the magnitude, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:uprime} supplies it in closed
form.

The hard-core problems turn out to be one problem seen from three sides. Hitting
set and vertex cover differ only in their constraint, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hhs} against
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hvc}; core percolation is not a third model but the obstruction to
solving the second by leaf removal; and on a graph its threshold and the
hard-field instability of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kc} are the same point, $\ave{k}=e$.
Above cardinality two the core-free branch disappears and certification fails
with it.

For hitting set the hard-field limit, Eq.~\eqref{eq:hs}, is complete only at
cardinality two: its order-reversing structure is general and $\ave{k}(c-1)=e$
is exact for the problem it describes, but the $O(1)$ part of the fields that it
discards matters as soon as complexes exceed cardinality two. Keeping the fields,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft}, repairs both symptoms --- disjoint $3$-hyperedges return
$1/3$ rather than $1/2$, and the regular solution of Ref.~\cite{MT07} is
recovered including the fractional $\mu/L$ in $h_{\mathrm{RS}}$ that no integer
ansatz can carry --- while extending it to the chy-degree distributions, mixed
cardinalities and correlated layers a regular ansatz cannot reach. It remains a
replica-symmetric calculation: where Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy} turns negative the
answer is an underestimate and one-step breaking is required, and the iteration
announces this by ceasing to converge.

The construction has one further limit, and it is structural rather than
technical. A chygraph is treelike only when its complexes meet in at most one
node. On hyperbolic random graphs the maximal cliques share edges, and the
measurement of Sec.~\ref{sec:regions} puts the cost at up to a third of the
core. What survives that cost is more than the qualitative separation from a
degree-matched control: the exponent with which the core grows is reproduced to
a few per cent, and the deficit is confined to intermediate density, where
overlap is largest, vanishing both when the graph is sparse enough that cliques
rarely meet and when it is dense enough that almost everything is core. Clustering is therefore not one obstruction but
a hierarchy of them --- promoting triangles removes triangles from the loop
structure and exposes the loops between triangles --- and whether that hierarchy
terminates usefully for a geometric ensemble, where motifs have no characteristic
size, is the question these measurements pose and this work does not settle.
